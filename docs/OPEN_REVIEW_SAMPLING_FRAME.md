# Open-review sampling frame

Checked 7 September 2026. The detailed evidence audits are [psychology and adjacent social science](research/journals-psychology.md), [Nature Portfolio and Royal Society](research/journals-nature-royal-society.md), and [broad and health-adjacent venues](research/journals-broad-and-health.md). The machine-readable inventory is [open-review-journals.json](research/open-review-journals.json).

## What can be sampled

There are three distinct populations:

1. **Conventional accepted papers with public reports and a public reviewed manuscript.** PeerJ (policy guarantees manuscript files for the applicable cohort), The BMJ/BMJ Open (previous manuscript versions are part of the stated history, subject to exceptions), and the inspected South African Journal of Science case are the clearest prepublication sources. Article-level checks remain mandatory.
2. **Conventional accepted papers with public reports but no guaranteed original submission.** Nature Portfolio titles, PLOS, Wiley transparent-review titles, BMC after March 2015, Collabra and Royal Society titles supply review text. For the pragmatic alpha, an audited pre-review preprint may enter the primary exploratory benchmark as a proxy; exact and proxy results must remain separate.
3. **Alternative review models.** F1000Research, Open Research Europe and current eLife expose versioned papers and reviews, but review occurs after publication or without conventional accept/reject decisions. Analyse these as a separate stratum.

This frame estimates performance against reviews of **accepted, and often author-opted-in, papers**. It does not estimate performance across all submissions or rejected papers. Mandatory and optional policies must be separate strata; optional publication creates selection on authors' willingness to disclose reviews.

## Sampling procedure

- Freeze the policy cohort before inspecting review quality: journal, article type, submission date, transparent-review status and any opt-in rule.
- Enumerate eligible records reproducibly from journal indexes or APIs. Draw a random accepted-paper sample within each stratum.
- Retain every substantive report for a sampled paper, including short or positive reports. Do not exclude a report after observing its agreement with an AI review.
- Require a hash of the manuscript and each report. Classify manuscript identity as `exact` only for an author- or journal-deposited file explicitly tied to the reviewed round, a byte-identical file, or complete normalized-main-text equality to a known submission with every difference documented as file metadata or rendering. Do not use a fuzzy similarity threshold to declare exactness. Classify a dated preprint alone as `temporal_proxy_unconfirmed`. Classify it as `substantively_audited_proxy` only after the checks below. A timestamp or label such as `R0`, `Version 0` or `Original manuscript` does not prove exact identity unless an actual tied manuscript file is present.
- Keep human reports hidden during AI generation and verification. Normalize only administrative boilerplate; preserve positive assessments and substantive headings.
- Record each model's declared knowledge cutoff and the paper's first-public date. Prefer papers and reports first made public after that cutoff where feasible; otherwise run and retain preregistered memorization probes against both the manuscript and each public human report, and report contamination risk. Persist backend configuration as provenance.
- Reviews run with web search, web fetching and sandboxed code execution. Every tool call (search query, fetched URL, shell command and truncated output) is logged per stage in `review.json`. The model is instructed not to consult peer reviews, editorial decisions, commentary or replies about the manuscript itself, and Claude refuses fetches from known review and commentary sites. Codex has no domain blocking, so for Codex runs the audit is the only check. Audit every benchmark run with `eval/audit_tool_use.py`; exclude flagged runs or report them with the audit reasons. Rankers and judges run without tools.
- Pretraining memory is a separate contamination risk that tool logging cannot detect; knowledge-cutoff dates and memorization probes address it. Treat public historical papers and reviews as a workflow and relative-comparison benchmark. For stronger validity, add a prospective blinded cohort of newly submitted manuscripts and later human reports obtained with author or journal permission, keeping those reports unavailable to the model until evaluation.
- Report results per paper and reviewer as well as pooled. Cluster uncertainty by paper.
- Construct a separately labelled **challenge panel** of detailed, methodologically demanding reviews. It tests difficult-case performance and must not be reported as population prevalence or mixed invisibly into the random sample.

## Admission states

`policy_guarantee` means an official current workflow promises the relevant artifact for the defined cohort. `verified_example` means a specific article bundle was directly inspected. `indexed_only` means a link or search record exists but the artifact was not retrieved. `access_blocked` records a retrieval failure, not absence. `unlocated` means this bounded search found no official public-review workflow; it is never a journal-wide proof of absence.

The most defensible **artifact-feasibility sample** is multi-journal: PeerJ's mandatory cohort, BMJ/BMJ Open cases with actual previous versions, and article-verified cases from other journals. It tests acquisition and scoring machinery, not performance in the target social-psychology population. Nature Portfolio and Royal Society reports add prestige and disciplinary breadth only after external manuscript matching. A psychology-focused feasibility stratum may add Meta-Psychology exact pairs while clearly reporting its niche status. F1000Research, Open Research Europe and eLife belong in sensitivity analyses of alternative review models.

Define the alpha's target population by substantive field and design before selecting journals: completed **quantitative** empirical social and personality psychology (experiments, surveys and quantitative measurement studies), with a declared publication window such as 2023–2025. Then enumerate journal cohorts in that population and choose sample sizes after recording eligible counts. Prioritize field-relevant mainstream titles such as Nature Human Behaviour, Communications Psychology and participating personality journals. The primary exploratory sample may include both `exact` and `substantively_audited_proxy` manuscripts, with results reported separately by identity tier and journal-policy stratum. Report an exact-only analysis; never blend `temporal_proxy_unconfirmed` records into performance estimates. Obtaining more exact files may require a prospective author or journal partnership. No outreach has been authorized or attempted. Add Meta-Psychology exact pairs as a separately reported discipline-focused stratum. Fix any challenge panel before AI scoring and report it separately. Qualitative and mixed-method records such as the inspected South African Journal of Science case demonstrate archive availability, but belong to future discipline/method profiles rather than validation of the current quantitative alpha.

This is the proposed proxy protocol. The current `evaluation.load_corpus` gate and existing manifests still admit only records marked `exact_pre_review_version_verified`; they have not been relaxed. Implementing proxy admission requires explicit manifest fields for identity tier, version timestamp and day gap, audit result, mismatch reasons, and version-dependent point flags rather than reusing the exact-version boolean.

Perform topic, article-type and design eligibility screening from a frozen metadata export containing no review links or review text. Record screeners' decisions before opening article pages or retrieving bundles. Only then acquire and validate artifacts for included records; this prevents visible review quality from influencing inclusion.

## Pragmatic preprint matching

Use a preprint posted within **±7 days** of submission as a discovery heuristic, not proof of identity. Select the public version closest to the submission for the review round being evaluated. It must predate that round's earliest dated report or decision. For transfers, match each journal's reports only to the version submitted for that journal's round; earlier reviews are a separate comparison and are excluded unless their own manuscript version is matched. Do not rely blindly on the accepting journal's displayed received date. Record version-specific server timestamps, the gap in days, all candidate versions and the reason for selecting one. Landing-page creation or last-edited dates do not substitute for version timestamps.

Search integrated submission-linked deposits first. Nature Communications' [editorial process](https://www.nature.com/ncomms/submit/editorial-process) describes the In Review service, available since June 2020, which posts a preprint and displays peer-review status from the submission workflow; Nature Human Behaviour's [submission guidance](https://www.nature.com/nathumbehav/submission-guidelines) also links its preprint and In Review routes. Springer Nature says an In Review preprint appears after journal quality control or when the manuscript is sent for review, while a direct Research Square posting normally follows a different timeline. This provenance is stronger than date coincidence, but still does not make a deposited version `exact` unless the record explicitly ties that version to the first review round. [Springer Nature In Review timing](https://support.springernature.com/en/support/solutions/articles/6000271870-can-i-opt-my-journal-submission-into-preprinting-on-the-research-square-platform-)

For `substantively_audited_proxy`, compare title, author list, study count, sample sizes, design and conditions, primary outcomes, central numerical results and claims, tables/figures, supplement availability, and reviewer-cited pages/lines/figures/tables across the proxy, published version and review/rebuttal exchange. Admission requires positive concordance on authors, studies, samples, design and the original facts or claims referenced by reviewers—not merely absence of a detected difference. Final-paper values need not equal the proxy when the rebuttal or revision record explains the change; reviewer-driven revision is expected. If the record is too short to establish the core fields, assign `audit_inconclusive` and leave it `temporal_proxy_unconfirmed`. Unexplained differences that bear on the selected round trigger downgrade or exclusion. This audit happens after metadata-based inclusion is frozen, before the auditor sees any AI output, and is kept outside model generation. It may use human reports solely for version matching; reports must never enter model context. Retain every human report after admission.

If review comments or rebuttals reveal a substantial mismatch—such as a missing study, changed primary outcome, materially different sample, or changed central result—exclude the paper from same-version ranking, log the attrition reason, and do not spend model calls on it. Before generation, freeze a map of known differences between the proxy and selected reviewed version, without judging AI output. During scoring, apply that map symmetrically: human points dependent on absent proxy content and AI points dependent on proxy-only content become `version_dependent_unevaluable`. Use review-origin-blind adjudication where feasible. Minor unevaluable points are logged and qualify the ranking; if substantive comparability is lost, produce no same-version ranking rather than applying an arbitrary percentage cutoff. Report excluded-case counts and reasons.

Record the actual day gap for every proxy and always report the prespecified default ±7-day analysis plus a ±30-day sensitivity analysis when available. Day distance is evidence about identity, not a hard identity cutoff: an older version with stronger workflow linkage and a successful substantive audit may enter a separately labelled wider-window stratum. Memorization probes and outcomes must be stratified by identity tier because proxy timing and training exposure are confounded. Discuss the additional risk that a model recalls the later published version and thereby anticipates changes prompted by reviewers.

Observed discovery examples show why ±7 days cannot be an exclusion rule. The [Research Square v1](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-4892837/v1/32e535f1-510b-412e-b5ee-277f8cb068af.pdf) associated with [Druckman et al. in Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02358-4) was posted 27 August 2024, 17 days after the displayed received date of 10 August; its route has not been established as In Review, and it is a lead only because supplement completeness and substantive identity have not been audited. For [Barry et al. in Communications Psychology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00312-8), version-specific PsyArXiv metadata records [v1 on 17 March 2025](https://api.osf.io/v2/preprints/vsp5q_v1/) and [v2 on 5 August 2025](https://api.osf.io/v2/preprints/vsp5q_v2/), respectively 99 days before and 42 days after the displayed received date of 24 June 2025, leaving no ±7-day candidate. These records need workflow and content evidence, not automatic inclusion or exclusion from date distance.

PeerJ, The BMJ and BMJ Open can support a separate **artifact-feasibility sample** because their workflows make pairing tractable. That convenience sample is health/access weighted and is not the recommended validation population for ordinary social psychology. Do not set arbitrary equal-journal quotas until topic-eligible counts and review-publication rates have been enumerated.

The already inspected PeerJ 236 case is retained as a retrospective workflow diagnostic, outside any future preregistered population estimate. Its demotion followed inspection of the human comparator quality and therefore cannot be used as a neutral inclusion decision; its AI outputs and all four Version 0.1 human reports remain reportable. A future sample must freeze eligibility before either human-review quality or AI agreement is examined.

Before generation, derive a manuscript-only file and verify that it contains no referee report, decision letter, author response, editorial assessment, or review metadata. This matters for platforms whose downloadable PDF can append reviews (for example, reviewed-preprint/postpublication platforms) and for combined history PDFs such as the inspected South African Journal of Science example. Preserve the untouched source hash, derived manuscript hash, extraction page bounds and an automated plus manual contamination check. Never pass a combined history file to the review pipeline.
