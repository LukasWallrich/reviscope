# Naming Proposals

1. **Refine** — Evokes polishing and refinement, with an echo of "coarse" as its antonym. Simple, memorable. *Drawback:* very common word; hard to search; may feel generic.

2. **Gristmill** — Grain processed into usable material; suggests turning raw critique into something valuable. *Drawback:* slightly rustic; two syllables plus compound may be less sleek.

3. **Screenfold** — Papers folded and examined; subtle archival/manuscript feel. *Drawback:* "screen" may evoke displays, causing confusion with screeners or UI tools.

4. **Vettura** — From "vetting," softened into a name-like form. *Drawback:* invented word risks seeming gimmicky or too startup-ish.

5. **Aperta** — Latin for "open," nodding to open science and open-source lineage. Clean, pronounceable. *Drawback:* several projects use "aperta" already; openness claim may feel overreachy for a review tool.

6. **Sieve** — Modular filtering of evidence and critique; short and vivid. *Drawback:* implies rejection/filtering more than constructive review; negative connotation of "losing fine details."

7. **Graderift** — Combines grading and drift/rift, hinting at evaluation and divergence from human reviews. *Drawback:* awkward; the drift connotation may suggest unreliability.

8. **Palimpsest** — Layered manuscripts; suits evidence-grounded review against texts. Scholarly resonance. *Drawback:* hard to spell and pronounce; may seem pretentious.

9. **Facet** — Modular evaluation across multiple dimensions of a manuscript. *Drawback:* heavily used in tech (Facets, facets in ML); low distinctiveness.

10. **Moraine** — Rock ground down by glaciers; gravel imagery links to coarse while suggesting accumulated evidence. *Drawback:* geology metaphor may feel opaque; coldness.

11. **Provetta** — Italian for "sample/specimen," suggesting evidence-based sampling of claims. *Drawback:* foreign word may hinder recall; pronunciation varies.

12. **Textmark** — Marks and measures texts; plainly descriptive and credible. *Drawback:* sounds like a watermarking or text-mining utility, underselling the review pipeline.

---

## Top 3

1. **Refine** — Direct, meaningful opposite of "coarse," honoring the inspiration while signaling independence; memorable, pronounceable, credible; makes no quality overclaim (refinement is a process, not a verdict).
2. **Gristmill** — Evocative of modular, systematic processing into scholarly value; distinctive and warm without hype; the grain link to coarse is subtle rather than derivative.
3. **Aperta** — Best fit with open-science values and the open-source community; elegant and academic; slight tension with the openness claim is outweighed by its scholarly tone.
