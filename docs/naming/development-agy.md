## Answer

Here are 8 proposed names designed around development, constructive momentum, and grounded verification—deliberately steering clear of grain metaphors, technical relics, Latin arcana, and clunky modular compounds—followed by the top 2 recommendations.

---

### 1. Soundcheck
* **Concept:** The supportive, private rehearsal musicians use to test levels and catch balance issues before stepping onto the public stage.
* **Promise:** Reclaims the notion of a "check" from bureaucratic gatekeeping and transforms it into an empowering, author-centric calibration. It signals a safe environment to stress-test claims, verify empirical evidence, and tune arguments before actual peer review.
* **Drawback:** Its musical roots may initially suggest audio or podcast production software if encountered without context.

### 2. Stride
* **Concept:** Purposeful, confident, forward momentum.
* **Promise:** Evokes progress and rhythm. It directly addresses the chronic academic anxiety of getting bogged down in manuscript revision, framing feedback as an engine for steady, dignified scholarly momentum.
* **Drawback:** Broad everyday word with potential overlap in fitness, productivity, or footwear branding.

### 3. Hone
* **Concept:** The craft of refining, sharpening, and bringing precision to an edge.
* **Promise:** Treats manuscript development as an authorial craft rather than an inspection. It conveys respect for the author’s existing work while promising to make arguments sharper, cleaner, and more defensible.
* **Drawback:** A very common term that risks feeling like a surface-level prose editor (e.g., Grammarly) rather than a deep methodological and theoretical review partner.

### 4. Traction
* **Concept:** The point where empirical grounding (grip) meets propulsion (fuel).
* **Promise:** Resonates with authors who feel they are "spinning their wheels" on a complex draft. It implies that rigorous evidence provides the firm purchase needed to drive the manuscript forward.
* **Drawback:** Has strong startup and venture-capital connotations ("seeking traction"), which some social-science scholars may find overly commercial.

### 5. Stoke
* **Concept:** Feeding fuel to a fire to generate sustained warmth, energy, and light.
* **Promise:** Leans directly into the "fuel" motif. It frames the author’s draft as an active intellectual spark that the tool fuels with constructive substance, reigniting enthusiasm for stalled projects.
* **Drawback:** Carries colloquial slang associations ("stoked") as well as potential negative connotations in media contexts ("stoking controversy").

### 6. Trellis
* **Concept:** An open framework that supports living vines as they branch, climb, and bear fruit.
* **Promise:** Deeply constructive, warm, and non-coercive. It promises structural feedback that supports organic theoretical growth without forcing the author's work into an artificial, rigid box.
* **Drawback:** The horticultural metaphor may feel too soft or decorative to researchers looking for hard-nosed empirical and statistical review.

### 7. Scaffold
* **Concept:** Grounded in developmental psychology and educational theory (Bruner/Vygotsky); temporary structures that enable creators to achieve higher complexity.
* **Promise:** Instant intellectual credibility among social scientists. It signals an evidence-based pedagogical approach—supporting the author during construction without claiming ownership of the final edifice.
* **Drawback:** The physical association with construction sites can feel a bit utilitarian, rigid, and industrial.

### 8. Rally
* **Concept:** Mobilizing evidence, gathering collective strength, and renewing energy to cross the finish line.
* **Promise:** Energetic, warm, and collegiate. It frames peer review revision not as an adversarial gauntlet, but as a rallying of evidence and clarity that turns a vulnerable manuscript into an undeniable paper.
* **Drawback:** Can feel slightly sports-adjacent or political depending on the audience.

---

### Top 2 Recommendations

1. **Soundcheck** (Best for the *check / soundness / calibration* angle)
   * **Why:** It solves the dilemma of "check" by stripping away the punitive, red-pen baggage of evaluation. A soundcheck is inherently collaborative, author-facing, and pre-emptive—it exists purely to make the performer look and sound their absolute best when the curtain goes up.

2. **Stride** (Best for the *development / momentum / fuel* angle)
   * **Why:** Short, warm, and distinctly non-judgmental. It captures the psychological relief of moving smoothly through a draft with confidence, promising authors that rigorous feedback will propel their work rather than slow it down.
