# Dual-use naming round — Sol

The product serves two legitimate contexts: researchers strengthening work in progress, and peer reviewers preparing an evidence-based assessment. The descriptor should state both uses even when the name is abstract.

## Eight candidates

### 1. Claimwise

**Why it fits:** The system examines a manuscript claim by claim and asks whether design, analysis, evidence and interpretation support each one. “Wise” suggests judgement without implying an automated verdict.

**Descriptor:** *Claim-aware manuscript assessment for researchers and peer reviewers.*

**Downside:** It may sound like a fact-checker unless the product presentation also shows study-level and methodological review.

### 2. PeerScope

**Why it fits:** “Peer” anchors the product in scholarly evaluation; “scope” suggests examining a manuscript through several disciplinary lenses. An author can use the same peer-review perspective before submission.

**Descriptor:** *Structured manuscript critique for authors and reviewers.*

**Downside:** “Peer” may initially suggest reviewer-only use, and the name is composed of common terms that may already be used elsewhere.

### 3. Reviewcraft

**Why it fits:** The name treats rigorous review as a skilled practice. It works for crafting a reviewer report and for using that report to improve a manuscript.

**Descriptor:** *Evidence-grounded review and manuscript development across disciplines.*

**Downside:** It foregrounds the review output more than statistical verification or source anchoring.

### 4. StudyLens

**Why it fits:** The tool applies several lenses to each study—design, measurement, inference, interpretation and reporting. The metaphor works during development and external review.

**Descriptor:** *Multi-lens assessment for research in development and review.*

**Downside:** “Lens” is widely used in software and consulting names, and it can sound observational rather than diagnostic.

### 5. Proofline

**Why it fits:** “Proof” evokes evidence and verification; “line” evokes traceable source anchors and a clear line from claim to support. It is concise and discipline-neutral.

**Descriptor:** *Traceable evidence checks and constructive manuscript review.*

**Downside:** In publishing, “proof” can mean typeset copy, so some users may expect proofreading rather than research assessment.

### 6. Research Soundboard

**Why it fits:** Authors use a soundboard to test an argument, while reviewers use it to develop a considered critique. The name conveys dialogue and constructive challenge.

**Descriptor:** *A rigorous sounding board for writing and reviewing research.*

**Downside:** It sounds approachable but less technically exacting, and the two-word name is longer.

### 7. Touchstone

**Why it fits:** A touchstone is a test of quality or authenticity. That applies equally to an author's internal assessment and a reviewer's evaluation.

**Descriptor:** *A transparent test of research claims, methods and reporting.*

**Downside:** The metaphor is broad, the word has many existing uses, and it does not signal manuscripts or peer review on its own.

### 8. Fuel

**Why it fits:** Framed neutrally, feedback fuels both stronger manuscripts and sharper peer review. It is short, positive and not tied to one discipline or workflow role.

**Descriptor:** *Evidence for stronger manuscripts and better-informed peer review.*

**Downside:** The name does not itself communicate assessment, scholarship, traceability or caution. It is highly generic, and “fuel” still leans toward progress and development more than evaluation. The descriptor would have to do substantial explanatory work.

## Top three

1. **Claimwise** — strongest connection to the product's evidence-centred unit of work, while remaining usable by authors and reviewers.
2. **PeerScope** — clearest compact signal of scholarly review and modular analytical lenses.
3. **Reviewcraft** — most human and practice-oriented; it presents the tool as support for thoughtful assessment rather than an automated gatekeeper.

`Claimwise` is the best working name. A practical full lockup would be **Claimwise — claim-aware manuscript assessment for researchers and peer reviewers**. `PeerScope` is stronger if immediate category recognition matters more than balanced author/reviewer connotations. `Reviewcraft` is strongest if the product should emphasize judgement and constructive prose over verification machinery.

These are naming concepts, not legal, domain or trademark clearance findings.
