### Proposed Names (8)

1. **Fuel**
   - **Promise**: Short, warm, and energetic. Suggests the tool provides the necessary energy to drive research improvement forward—like fuel for a journey. It’s positive and action-oriented, avoiding judgment.
   - **Drawback**: May feel too generic or metaphorical; lacks direct connection to rigorous review. Could imply only “power” rather than structured, evidence-based feedback.

2. **Spark**
   - **Promise**: Evokes ignition and creativity—a friendly nudge that starts a process of development. Warm and memorable, it suggests constructive inspiration rather than correction.
   - **Drawback**: Might be perceived as too vague or whimsical for a tool meant to enforce rigorous standards. Could downplay the systematic, evidence-grounded nature of the review.

3. **Nudge**
   - **Promise**: Borrows from behavioral economics; implies a gentle, supportive push toward improvement without force or judgment. Warm, approachable, and credible in social science contexts.
   - **Drawback**: Risks being seen as too soft or informal for rigorous manuscript review. May not convey the depth of evidence-based critique needed.

4. **Anchor**
   - **Promise**: Suggests stability and grounding in evidence—keeping the research steady and connected to solid foundations. Short, credible, and reassuring.
   - **Drawback**: Can feel static (anchoring prevents movement) rather than developmental. Might imply a tool that holds work back or judges it against fixed standards.

5. **Lens**
   - **Promise**: Implies clarity and focus—helping authors see their work from a new, more rigorous perspective. Warm, neutral, and memorable; evokes careful examination without gatekeeping.
   - **Drawback**: Passive; a lens simply shows, it doesn’t act. Could be interpreted as only providing a view, not active feedback or improvement steps.

6. **Pulse**
   - **Promise**: Suggests vitality and ongoing health checks—a tool that monitors the life of a manuscript and offers timely, constructive input. Warm, organic, and forward-looking.
   - **Drawback**: May feel too medical or biological; might not clearly communicate the analytical, evidence-based nature of the review process.

7. **Craft**
   - **Promise**: Evokes careful, deliberate improvement—shaping research with skill and attention. Warm, credible, and respectful of the author’s process. Aligns with development and constructive feedback.
   - **Drawback**: Could be associated with artistic writing rather than social-science rigor. Might imply manual, hands-on work rather than systematic, tool-driven review.

8. **Progress**
   - **Promise**: Directly conveys forward movement and development—exactly what authors seek. Short, warm, and universally positive. No gatekeeping or judgment; just a focus on getting better.
   - **Drawback**: Very broad; could be used for any improvement tool, lacking specificity. Might not immediately signal that it’s a manuscript review tool, especially one grounded in evidence.

---

### Top 2 Recommendations

1. **Fuel** – Best captures the “feel of ‘fuel’” the user mentioned. It’s short, warm, and memorable, while implying active, constructive energy rather than judgment. The drawback (genericness) can be mitigated by clear branding and context.

2. **Progress** – Strongly aligns with the “development, progress” direction. It’s universally understood, warm, and credible. Its broadness is a minor issue, but the word itself is powerful and motivating for authors seeking improvement.
