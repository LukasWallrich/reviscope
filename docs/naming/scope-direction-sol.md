# Naming exploration: beyond VeriScope

These names frame the product as a disciplined way to examine research. They work for authors improving a manuscript and reviewers preparing an assessment, without claiming to certify truth.

## Leading options

### ReviewScope

The clearest dual-use name. “Review” covers both reviewing one’s own work and peer review; “scope” suggests a structured view across design, evidence, inference and reporting. It sounds like a professional research tool rather than an authority that issues verdicts.

**Descriptor:** Structured manuscript review for authors and peer reviewers.

**Downside:** Descriptive rather than distinctive, and likely to have more naming collisions than coined alternatives.

### Reviso

Short, pronounceable and rooted in both reviewing and revising. It naturally spans assessment and development while making no claim of objectivity. The softer name leaves room for profiles across disciplines.

**Descriptor:** Evidence-aware review and revision for research manuscripts.

**Downside:** Its meaning is less immediate in English and it may be mistaken for a general writing or document-revision product.

### Refract

Suggests examining the same manuscript through several disciplinary lenses. That fits a modular system particularly well: methods, measurement, inference and reporting each reveal a different aspect of the work. It emphasizes perspective rather than final truth.

**Descriptor:** Multiple expert perspectives on research in review.

**Downside:** The manuscript-review function needs explanation on first encounter, and the optical metaphor may feel abstract.

## Other viable directions

### ReviewLens

Directly communicates careful examination from several viewpoints and works equally well before submission and during peer review.

**Descriptor:** A structured lens for developing and reviewing research.

**Downside:** “Lens” is common in analytics and accessibility products, reducing distinctiveness.

### ResearchScope

Broad, discipline-neutral and immediately credible. It can grow beyond manuscript critique into preregistration, supplements and other research artifacts.

**Descriptor:** Structured assessment across the research lifecycle.

**Downside:** Very broad and potentially mistaken for a literature-search or research-management product.

### Manuscope

A compact blend of manuscript and scope. It signals close inspection while remaining neutral about whether the user is an author or reviewer.

**Descriptor:** Close, evidence-linked examination of research manuscripts.

**Downside:** Coined and slightly clinical sounding; pronunciation and spelling may need reinforcement.

### Perspect

Centers critical perspective and suggests an active examination rather than automated certification. It can comfortably support multiple disciplinary profiles.

**Descriptor:** Critical perspectives for stronger research and review.

**Downside:** The truncated spelling may feel like a startup construction and does not immediately signal manuscripts.

### ReviewArc

Evokes the full path from work in progress through peer assessment and revision. It is constructive without privileging authors over reviewers.

**Descriptor:** Structured review from working draft to peer assessment.

**Downside:** It communicates process more strongly than evidence verification or methodological depth.

## Recommendation

1. **ReviewScope** — strongest immediate comprehension and the most balanced author/reviewer framing.
2. **Reviso** — strongest compact brand if a less literal name is preferred.
3. **Refract** — strongest expression of the modular, multiple-perspective product design.

**VeriScope** remains appealing, but “Veri” can imply verification or certified correctness. If retained, its descriptor should soften that implication: **“Structured perspectives for developing and reviewing research.”**
