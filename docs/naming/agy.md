### Naming Proposals

1. **Grain**
   * **Rationale:** A direct, organic evolution from *coarse* (moving from coarse texture to granular inspection). Evokes fine-grained analysis, the internal texture of academic argumentation, and checking claims against textual evidence down to the smallest detail.
   * **Likely Drawbacks:** As a common noun, it faces naming collisions with existing software and tools across general tech domains.

2. **Winnow**
   * **Rationale:** Refers to the traditional process of separating the grain from the chaff. Serves as a subtle thematic bridge to grain/coarseness while precisely describing the epistemic function of peer review: separating substantive, grounded critique from noise.
   * **Likely Drawbacks:** Slightly literary/archaic vocabulary that may be less instantly recognized by non-native English speakers.

3. **Assay**
   * **Rationale:** A rigorous, methodical test or analysis to determine the composition, quality, and validity of an object or argument. Credible and scientific without overpromising absolute truth or infallibility.
   * **Likely Drawbacks:** Strongly associated with wet-lab biochemistry and metallurgy, which might feel slightly foreign to qualitative social scientists.

4. **Hone**
   * **Rationale:** To hone means to sharpen, refine, or smooth through friction on a fine-grained stone. Frame the pipeline as a constructive instrument that sharpens manuscripts and critiques rather than an adversarial judge.
   * **Likely Drawbacks:** Very short and widely used in productivity and SaaS branding; lacks specific connotations of manuscript review.

5. **Recensio**
   * **Rationale:** Classical scholarly term for the systematic collation and critical examination of a manuscript and its sources. Conveys deep academic legitimacy and describes the exact task of verifying criticisms directly against text.
   * **Likely Drawbacks:** Latinate phrasing can feel formal, conservative, or humanities-skewed rather than modern and computational.

6. **Sieve**
   * **Rationale:** Directly references particle size and modular filtering, echoing the mechanics of *coarse*. Represents passing claims and criticisms through modular thresholds to catch ungrounded statements.
   * **Likely Drawbacks:** Irregular spelling (`i-e-v-e`) and existing associations with classic computer science algorithms (e.g., Sieve of Eratosthenes).

7. **Strata**
   * **Rationale:** References layers, sedimentation, and geological/social stratification. Captures both the modular, multi-stage architecture of the pipeline and themes familiar to social science research.
   * **Likely Drawbacks:** Strongly linked to data conferences and general analytics brands; primarily suggests static layering rather than dynamic evaluation.

8. **Grist**
   * **Rationale:** Grain prepared for grinding ("grist for the mill"). Bridges the coarse grain heritage with the concept of taking raw manuscript drafts and turning them into refined, usable scholarly feedback.
   * **Likely Drawbacks:** Idiomatic English that does not translate easily globally; shares a name with an established open-source data tool.

9. **Tessera**
   * **Rationale:** An individual stone or tile used in a mosaic (texture and modularity), as well as a historical token used in antiquity for voting, balloting, and verification.
   * **Likely Drawbacks:** Abstract and metaphorical; does not immediately signal text, review, or academic workflows.

10. **Collatio**
    * **Rationale:** The classical practice of critical, side-by-side textual comparison to detect discrepancies and verify claims. Accurately describes checking reviewers' points against manuscript text.
    * **Likely Drawbacks:** Can sound somewhat clerical or administrative rather than analytical.

11. **Plumb**
    * **Rationale:** Metaphor of the plumb line: testing vertical alignment, depth, and grounding against a reference point. Conveys structural integrity checking without making grandiose claims of infallibility.
    * **Likely Drawbacks:** Monosyllabic and abrupt; risks immediate association with domestic plumbing.

12. **Mesh**
    * **Rationale:** Evokes both a physical filter (particle thresholding, linking to *coarse*) and an interconnected network of modular checks and human-AI evaluations.
    * **Likely Drawbacks:** Highly overloaded term across computing (mesh networks, 3D polygon meshes, MeSH medical headings).

## Answer

### Top 3 Recommendations

1. **Winnow**
   * **Reasons:** It offers the most accurate metaphor for peer review: sifting substantive, evidence-grounded criticism from irrelevant noise. It honors the thematic connection to *coarse* (grain vs. chaff) without being derivative, avoids all exaggerated claims of authority, and carries an understated, credible scholarly tone.

2. **Grain**
   * **Reasons:** The cleanest and most natural progression from *coarse* (shifting focus from rough/unrefined to granular rigor). It is short, memorable, easy to pronounce in international academic contexts, and works exceptionally well as a developer-friendly command-line and package name (e.g., `grain review`, `import grain`).

3. **Assay**
   * **Reasons:** It explicitly frames the pipeline as a standardized, modular test of evidence and claim grounding rather than an arbiter of absolute truth. It sounds disciplined, highly credible across both social sciences and STEM, and neatly conveys evaluation against benchmarks without jargon.
