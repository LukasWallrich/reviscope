## Historical first-round shortlist — rejected

### Marginwork

The strongest balance of distinctiveness, meaning, and restraint. It presents review as careful scholarly work alongside a manuscript, accommodates both generated criticism and verification, and does not imply that the system determines truth. “Work” also fits an extensible tool rather than a single model.

**Collision check:** Exact-name searches found no obvious academic or AI software product. This is encouraging, not clearance. The main drawback is that it may initially sound like page-layout work or financial margin analysis.

### GrainReview

The clearest qualified descendant of the coarse/grain metaphor. Adding “Review” makes the function immediately legible and avoids trying to own the extremely crowded word “Grain.” It should work as both a project and command name.

**Collision check:** Exact-name searches surfaced generic agricultural “grain review” uses rather than an obvious software product. `Grain` alone is occupied by languages, packages, and AI or meeting products, so the qualifier is essential. The name remains more visibly tied to coarse than the other finalists.

### Text & Test

The best plain-language description of the method: close reading paired with claim, quotation, and statistical checks. It travels across disciplines and makes no quality guarantee. The ampersand gives it an editorial identity; a package or CLI would need a normalized form such as `textandtest`.

**Collision check:** The phrase is generic and appears in educational and software-testing contexts. No single collision dominates, but searchability and package naming are weaker than Marginwork.

### Evidence Margin

Directly conveys source-grounded commentary and the boundary between supported and unresolved criticism. It also preserves the annotation metaphor without restricting the system to one discipline.

**Collision check:** “Evidence margin” appears as a technical phrase in research rather than an obvious exact-name product. Its generic components may make protection and discovery difficult, and the name underplays peer review unless paired with a descriptor.

### Review Loom

The best architectural metaphor in the ensemble: discipline profiles, review modules, verification, and editorial synthesis are woven into a coherent output. It is pronounceable and constructive.

**Collision check:** “Loom” is strongly associated with the video product and appears widely in software names; “review loom” also risks being read as reviewing Loom videos. Keep it only if product clarity outweighs that association.

## Names removed after collision checks

- **Scholion / Scholium:** Scholion AI already exists, while Scholium is used by several active AI research, study, and academic-publishing products.
- **Groundline:** an existing product at `usegroundline.com` works with evidence and is too close in both name and concept.
- **Stet:** multiple current AI writing, annotation, and agent-evaluation products use the exact name, including `getstet.app`, `stet.sh`, and `stet.fyi`.
- **Winnow:** crowded in AI and software, including an existing Winnow AI repository.
- **Grist / Gristmill:** Grist is an established data tool and Gristmill is an existing software project.
- **Grain / Granule:** both are already used by programming languages or packages; Grain is also broad and difficult to search.
- **Recensio:** used by a current AI review-response product and an established scholarly-review platform.
- **Assay, Refine, Facet, Mesh, Sieve:** highly occupied generic terms whose search results do not reliably identify this product category.

## Second-round development direction

The follow-up ensemble shifted from scholarly annotation and grain metaphors toward helping authors develop research:

- **Fuel** communicates momentum and the purpose of helping work progress. It is warm and memorable, but highly generic and already crowded across research and product spaces.
- **Soundcheck** frames rigorous feedback as a constructive rehearsal before public scrutiny. Its meaning fits the author-support goal, but it is occupied by Spotify's developer-quality product and strongly associated with audio.
- **Check** states the evidence-verification function most clearly. It is narrower and cooler than the broader development promise, and as a common word would be difficult to distinguish or clear.

These are descriptions of a direction, not finalists. No name has been selected or cleared, and no repository, package, command, profile, or public report has been renamed. The source outputs are preserved in `development-brief.md` and `development-{agy,deepseek,glm,sol}.md`.
