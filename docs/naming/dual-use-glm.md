# 8 Name Proposals

**1. Verdictwork**
- *Rationale:* Pairs the evidentiary weight of a "verdict" with the labor implied by "work" — this is a process, not a rubber stamp. Feels serious without editorial authority.
- *Dual-use descriptor:* "Structured critique labor for manuscripts at any stage, by anyone, for any reader."
- *Downside:* "Verdict" hints at judgment already rendered — an author mid-draft may feel the word presumes guilt before trial.

**2. Charcoal**
- *Rationale:* Charcoal is what fuel becomes after burning — material refined by combustion, still usable for drawing and sketching (drafting). It quietly honors the fuel idea the user liked, without the one-sided framing.
- *Dual-use descriptor:* "Burns a manuscript down to its evidence; what remains is drawable."
- *Downside:* Slightly oblique; users may not get the fuel connection, and "charcoal" can read as gray/sooty in branding.

**3. Crosscheck**
- *Rationale:* Plain, memorable, and inherently symmetrical — one party's claim is checked across another source. Works identically whether the claim is yours or someone else's.
- *Dual-use descriptor:* "Independent claims, checked from both directions."
- *Downside:* Borrowed from aviation; may feel generic, and it emphasizes verification over the critique-generation half of the pipeline.

**4. Loadstone**
- *Rationale:* A loadstone tests weight-bearing; also a "lodestone" that orients. Double meaning: how much load a paper's argument can carry, and where reviewers should anchor their evaluation.
- *Dual-use descriptor:* "Find what your argument bears, and where others' claims actually hold."
- *Downside:* The two spellings/meanings may blur; puns invite eye-rolls in academic settings.

**5. Kindling**
- *Rationale:* Fuel, reconsidered neutrally — kindling starts the fire regardless of who strikes it. Also connotes the small, precise pieces (critiques) that precede a larger burn (revision, review).
- *Dual-use descriptor:* "Small, dry, precise critiques — ready for either a rewrite or a review."
- *Downside:* Kindling is consumed in use; may imply critiques are disposable rather than rigorous artifacts.

**6. Benchtest**
- *Rationale:* A bench test is run on a component before or after installation — same apparatus, same rigor, regardless of who built it. Strongly evokes modular engineering, matching the pipeline's modularity.
- *Dual-use descriptor:* "Put any manuscript on the bench: same tests for builders and inspectors."
- *Downside:* Reads as engineering jargon; humanities disciplines may feel it's not meant for them.

**7. Undergird**
- *Rationale:* Names the pipeline's actual function — testing what undergirds a claim. Verb-form names (like Slack, Notion) are short and memorable; it works as both action and identity.
- *Dual-use descriptor:* "Check what holds the argument up — yours or anyone's."
- *Downside:* Semantically close to "gut check"/foundation metaphors that are already overused in peer-review software pitches.

**8. Furnace Review / Furnace**
- *Rationale:* A furnace is neutral infrastructure — heat applied to whatever goes in; both the smith and the inspector use the same furnace. Keeps the fuel/check/development family the user gestured at, reframed as shared equipment rather than author service.
- *Dual-use descriptor:* "Shared heat: the same furnace tests the blade and the finished lock."
- *Downside:* Metaphor implies stress-testing only; may sound harsh to authors, and "Furnace" alone is vague on academic context.

# Top 3, Ranked

**1. Crosscheck.** The strongest fit to the dual-use constraint: checking is inherently direction-neutral, and the name needs no explanation. Tradeoff: it's the least distinctive and least "inventive" — it buys credibility and symmetry at the cost of personality, and it underplays the generative-critique side.

**2. Charcoal.** Best balance of invention and meaning: it salvages the fuel direction the user liked, strips the one-sided framing, and carries a legitimate drafting connotation that serves authors without excluding reviewers. Tradeoff: the metaphor takes a sentence to land, and the sooty connotation is a branding gamble.

**3. Benchtest.** The most honest description of what a modular pipeline actually does — same rig, different users. Tradeoff: its engineering register may alienate humanities fields, which is exactly where "tailored across disciplines" needs to flex; it's the riskiest of the three for cross-disciplinary adoption.
