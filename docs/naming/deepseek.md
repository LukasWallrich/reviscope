## Proposals

1. **Stet**
   Rationale: Classic editorial/proofreading instruction meaning “let it stand”; fits a tool that decides whether a critique is justified or the manuscript should remain unchanged.
   Drawback: Outside editing circles, the meaning is obscure.

2. **Grain**
   Rationale: Evokes granular analysis, “coarse/fine” distinctions, and the skeptical instinct to “take with a grain of salt.”
   Drawback: Common word with many unrelated associations.

3. **Grounds**
   Rationale: Points to the evidentiary basis for a claim or critique; the coffee-grounds imagery also nods to “coarse.”
   Drawback: Ambiguous with “grounds” as property or coffee.

4. **Scholium**
   Rationale: A scholarly note attached to a text or proposition; suggests rigorous, citable commentary.
   Drawback: Unfamiliar to most readers; may feel elitist.

5. **Collation**
   Rationale: Conveys systematic comparison of claims, quotes, and critiques against source manuscripts.
   Drawback: Textual-criticism and office-printer baggage; not especially catchy.

6. **Marginalia**
   Rationale: Captures peer review as annotations in the margins, closely tied to specific passages.
   Drawback: Long, and not obviously an automated or AI-driven system.

7. **Winnow**
   Rationale: Separates stronger evidence and criticisms from weaker ones; grain/vs-chaff imagery connects subtly to “coarse.”
   Drawback: Agricultural metaphor may feel less academic.

8. **Gauge**
   Rationale: Suggests measuring how well a criticism aligns with the manuscript and human review norms.
   Drawback: Implies precision that may overpromise; generic sound.

9. **Recension**
   Rationale: A formal term for critical review and revision of a text, conveying scholarly care.
   Drawback: Obscure and often tied specifically to old manuscript editing.

10. **Vetter**
   Rationale: A concise name for “one who vets”; the process is clear and the verb is standard in review contexts.
   Drawback: Sounds like “veterinarian” or a person, not a platform.

11. **Texture**
   Rationale: Refers to coarse/fine texture, and shares the Latin root “text/texere” with “text” — a nice fit for manuscript review.
   Drawback: Vague; does not obviously signal peer review.

12. **Roughcut**
   Rationale: A subtle “coarse” synonym paired with editorial cutting; fits an early-stage modular pipeline.
   Drawback: Feels unfinished or informal for an academic product.

## Top 3

1. **Stet** — It captures the core action: letting a manuscript stand unless a critique holds up. It is short, credible, and rooted in editorial practice, making it memorable for an academic audience.

2. **Grounds** — It names the central requirement of evidence-grounded review, and the “coarse/fine grounds” listening to the old working name without copying it.

3. **Grain** — It works at multiple levels: “grain” as granularity and texture, “a grain of salt” as critical caution, and a quiet homage to “coarse.”
