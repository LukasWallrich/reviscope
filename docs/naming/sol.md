# Sol naming slate

## Top three

1. **Scholion** — An old term for a critical or explanatory note on a text. It sounds academic, is pronounceable (`SKOH-lee-on`), and fits a system that produces evidence-linked commentary rather than claiming to pronounce truth. The singular form feels like a product; “scholia” could naturally name its findings.
2. **Groundline** — Suggests the evidential line beneath every criticism and the baseline against which reviews are evaluated. It is clear outside academia, works across disciplines, and leaves room for modules such as Groundline Psychology or Groundline Methods.
3. **Review Loom** — Conveys a pipeline that weaves separate disciplinary, methodological, verification, and editorial components into one review. It is memorable, easy to say, and makes modularity visible without implying infallibility.

## Full slate

1. **Scholion** — Critical notes anchored to a source text; compact and distinctly scholarly.
2. **Groundline** — Evidence grounding plus a transparent evaluation baseline.
3. **Review Loom** — Modular review components woven into a coherent whole.
4. **Marginwork** — Serious analytical work in the tradition of scholarly marginal commentary.
5. **Proofmark** — Marks claims for scrutiny and records supporting evidence; “proof” refers to examination, not guaranteed correctness.
6. **Scholar’s Bench** — A workbench for assembling and testing discipline-specific reviews.
7. **Review Atlas** — Maps a manuscript’s studies, claims, evidence, and concerns across disciplines.
8. **Text & Test** — A direct pairing of close reading with verification and statistical checks.
9. **Critique Kit** — A modular toolkit name with an approachable, extensible feel.
10. **Evidence Margin** — Places traceable evidence beside each review claim and also evokes scholarly annotation.
11. **Peerframe** — Frames a manuscript through explicit disciplinary criteria and comparison with peer review.
12. **Method Sift** — Separates consequential methodological issues from routine or unsupported criticism.

## Recommendation

**Scholion** is the strongest standalone identity. It has scholarly lineage, does not tie the system to one discipline, and describes the output modestly: a critical note grounded in a text. **Groundline** is the clearest choice if immediate intelligibility matters more than distinctiveness. **Review Loom** best communicates the architecture and future profile ecosystem.

Before adoption, the finalists should receive ordinary trademark, package-name, repository, and domain checks. This slate is a naming proposal, not a clearance search.
