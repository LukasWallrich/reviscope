# Independent naming brief

Propose exactly eight fresh names for a modular, cross-disciplinary academic manuscript assessment tool. The tool serves two equally important uses:

1. authors use it to develop and improve research in progress; and
2. peer reviewers use it to prepare rigorous, evidence-grounded reviews and verification.

The name and descriptor must communicate this dual use. Do not frame the product only as author coaching, manuscript development, journal gatekeeping, or reviewer automation.

The system begins in quantitative social science but is designed to support tailored discipline profiles. It generates prioritized substantive feedback, anchors claims in manuscript evidence, checks selected statistical claims, verifies proposed findings and remedies, and can be evaluated against human reviews.

Constraints:

- Give eight names, then select and rank a top three.
- For every name, provide a short descriptor/tagline and one sentence explaining what promise the name makes.
- Names should be short, credible, memorable, and pronounceable.
- Avoid forced acronyms and exaggerated claims about truth, correctness, certainty, or peer-review equivalence.
- The user rejected these names and directions: coarse, Grain, Marginwork, GrainReview, Text & Test, EvidenceMargin, and ReviewLoom. Do not recycle or lightly modify them.
- The user suggested the direction of “Fuel” and “Check,” but “Fuel — constructive review for research in progress” was rejected because it sounded author-only and appeared to rule out reviewer use. Reconsider Fuel neutrally: it may be retained, changed, or rejected, but any proposed descriptor must cover authors and peer reviewers equally.
- Do not make legal, trademark, domain, package-name, or availability assertions; no clearance research is requested.
- Generate genuinely fresh options rather than commenting mainly on the rejected slate.

Return only the requested naming work. End your response with the answer itself under a heading `## Answer`.
