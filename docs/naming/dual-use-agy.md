## Answer

### Eight Candidate Names

1. **Crosscheck**
   * **Descriptor:** Evidence-grounded manuscript verification and critique for authors and peer reviewers.
   * **Promise:** Crosscheck promises a disciplined, side-by-side verification of claims against data and text, giving authors a pre-submission diagnostic and reviewers an evidence base for their reports.

2. **Keystone**
   * **Descriptor:** Modular claim analysis and evidence verification for research in progress and peer review.
   * **Promise:** Keystone promises to identify and stress-test the central, load-bearing findings of a study to ensure the manuscript’s overarching conclusions rest on sound empirical ground.

3. **Fulcrum**
   * **Descriptor:** Substantive manuscript assessment and evidence balancing for authors and peer reviewers.
   * **Promise:** Fulcrum promises to pinpoint the pivotal empirical and methodological points that determine whether a paper’s claims hold weight under scrutiny.

4. **Vantage**
   * **Descriptor:** Multi-angle manuscript critique and evidence checking for researchers and reviewers.
   * **Promise:** Vantage promises an objective, elevated analytical perspective that helps authors uncover blind spots before submission and helps reviewers ground their assessments in manuscript evidence.

5. **Caliber**
   * **Descriptor:** Evidence-anchored manuscript calibration for research development and peer review.
   * **Promise:** Caliber promises to calibrate empirical claims and statistical reporting against disciplined scholarly standards without asserting automated infallibility.

6. **Tenable**
   * **Descriptor:** Claim-level evidence verification and manuscript review for authors and evaluators.
   * **Promise:** Tenable promises to test whether empirical claims and methodological choices hold up under critical scrutiny, helping authors fortify arguments and reviewers substantiate critiques.

7. **Truss**
   * **Descriptor:** Structural manuscript evaluation and empirical verification for authors and peer reviewers.
   * **Promise:** Truss promises to stress-test the inferential framework supporting a manuscript, identifying structural weaknesses for authors to fix and reviewers to inspect.

8. **Fuel**
   * **Descriptor:** Substantive critique and empirical verification powering manuscript revision and peer review.
   * **Promise:** Fuel promises to supply the concrete evidence checks and prioritized feedback needed to drive manuscript improvement for authors and power rigorous evaluations for reviewers.

---

### Ranked Top Three

1. **Crosscheck**
   * **Why it ranks first:** It delivers on the “Check” direction with immediate scholarly credibility and perfect dual-use symmetry. Crosschecking is an established, non-bureaucratic practice that authors and peer reviewers perform identically: cross-referencing text against tables, claims against data, and findings against disciplinary standards. It avoids gatekeeping bias, claims no artificial authority, and clearly signals diagnostic rigor.

2. **Keystone**
   * **Why it ranks second:** It provides an intuitive structural metaphor centered on load-bearing claims. In quantitative social science and empirical inquiry, papers stand or fall on whether core findings and statistical arguments hold. Keystone tells authors where their drafts need reinforcement before submission, while giving reviewers a direct map of the empirical pillars that require verification.

3. **Fulcrum**
   * **Why it ranks third:** It represents balance and leverage rather than unilateral judgment. The name dignifies both sides of scholarly communication: authors use it to find the leverage points that elevate draft quality, while reviewers use it to weigh claims fairly against manuscript evidence.

#### Tradeoff Note on “Fuel”
While **Fuel** is short, energetic, and memorable, its underlying metaphor naturally leans toward propulsion, generation, and forward progress. Even with a carefully balanced dual-use descriptor, peer reviewers typically view their work through the lens of scrutiny, audit, and verification rather than providing "fuel." Names such as **Crosscheck** and **Keystone** align far more naturally with both sides of the workflow without requiring the descriptor to correct an inherent developmental tilt.
