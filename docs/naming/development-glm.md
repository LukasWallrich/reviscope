**1. Refine**
- *Promise:* Directly says what the tool does — improves manuscripts. Warm, humble, active. "Refine your paper" reads naturally.
- *Drawback:* Generic; many products use it, weak distinctiveness and hard to trademark/own as a category.

**2. Passages**
- *Promise:* Evokes moving a manuscript forward, plus "passages" of text — literary, gentle, social-science-flavored.
- *Drawback:* Ambiguous; could be a reading app or travel tool. Progress metaphor is subtle to the point of invisible.

**3. Bench**
- *Promise:* "Peer" without the gatekeeping — a workbench where work gets done. Credible, scholarly, one syllable.
- *Drawback:* Crowded space (bench science, Bench accounting); may read as lab-oriented, wrong field.

**4. Kiln**
- *Promise:* Craft metaphor — careful heat that strengthens rather than burns. Short, memorable, warm if slightly artisanal.
- *Drawback:* A kiln is for firing, i.e., judgment-by-fire imagery is one step from gatekeeping; meaning requires explanation.

**5. Proofer / Proofpoint Draft (call it "Proseproof" or just "Proofed" — pick "Proofer")**
Actually simplify: **Proofed**
- *Promise:* Familiar to academics (proofs, proofreading); signals rigor and checking without claiming infallibility.
- *Drawback:* Sounds like copyediting, not substantive methodological review — undersells the tool.

**6. Draftwise**
- *Promise:* "Wise" suggests thoughtful guidance across drafts; iterative improvement is baked in.
- *Drawback:* "-wise" suffix is a SaaS cliché; risks the compound-name feel the user dislikes.

**7. Ballast**
- *Promise:* What keeps a paper steady and evidence-grounded — credibility metaphor, nautical warmth, uncommon enough to own.
- *Drawback:* Ballast is weight that prevents capsizing — defensive/stabilizing, not developmental; may feel like criticism-adjacent.

**8. Forward**
- *Promise:* Pure progress framing: "move your research forward." Optimistic, warm, plain-language, no jargon.
- *Drawback:* Very common word, likely heavily taken; weak specificity — could be any productivity tool.

---

**Top 2:**

1. **Refine** — the truest name: it promises exactly what happens (iterative, constructive improvement), is warm and non-judgmental, and needs no explanation. Its genericness is its only real cost, and in a trust-driven category "honest plain word" may beat "distinctive coined word."

2. **Bench** — short, credible, scholarly-but-collaborative; recasts peer review as sitting alongside the author at a workbench rather than across a judging table. Best balance of warmth and academic seriousness, with decent distinctiveness if the space is checked carefully.
