# Development-oriented naming pass — Sol

## Brief

Name a modular tool that helps social-science authors develop and improve research through rigorous, evidence-grounded feedback. The name should feel supportive rather than gatekeeping, remain credible in academic settings, and avoid claims that the tool guarantees truth or quality. This pass deliberately sets aside all previous candidates and the coarse-inspired vocabulary.

## Options

1. **Temper** — Promises iterative strengthening through testing and revision. It conveys rigor without suggesting that the tool certifies the finished work. The metallurgical association is memorable, while “temper” also implies measured judgment.

2. **Draftwise** — Promises informed help while research is still open to change. It is warm and immediately understandable, although the “wise” ending makes a mild competence claim and should be presented as guidance rather than authority.

3. **Probe** — Promises focused questioning of claims, methods, and evidence. It is short and disciplinary-neutral, though slightly more clinical than warm.

4. **Workbench** — Promises a practical place where authors inspect and improve research. It supports a modular product architecture and emphasizes ongoing work, but it is less distinctive because the word is widely used in software.

5. **Headway** — Promises useful progress on a manuscript or study. It feels encouraging and avoids claiming correctness, though the connection to evidence checking is indirect.

6. **Sounding** — Promises a careful test of depth and hidden weaknesses, with feedback that helps authors navigate. The word has established exploratory meanings, but some users may first associate it with audio.

7. **Gauge** — Promises structured assessment that helps authors decide what needs attention. It is concise and credible, while deliberately stopping short of a pass/fail guarantee.

8. **Tend** — Promises attentive development of work over time. It is the warmest candidate and fits an author-support product, although its rigor and evidence-grounding would need to come from the tagline and visual identity.

## Best two

### 1. Temper

**Temper** best combines rigor, development, and restraint. Research is strengthened by exposing it to pressure and revising it; the result may improve without being declared flawless. The name works across disciplines and can comfortably cover modular activities such as methods checks, evidence verification, and substantive feedback.

Suggested descriptor: **Temper — rigorous feedback for research in development.**

### 2. Gauge

**Gauge** is the clearest compact name for an evidence-grounded assessment tool. It suggests measurement and diagnosis that inform the author's next step, rather than an editorial verdict. It is especially suitable if the interface foregrounds evidence coverage, uncertainty, and calibrated findings.

Suggested descriptor: **Gauge — evidence-grounded checks for stronger research.**

## Naming caution

These are concept proposals, not trademark, package-registry, or domain clearance. Their promises depend on the accompanying descriptor: **Temper** needs the research context made explicit, while **Gauge** needs language that keeps “measurement” from sounding like a single quality score.
