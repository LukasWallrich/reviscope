# Broad and health-adjacent open-review sources

Checked 7 September 2026. This is a purposive policy and article-artifact audit, not an exhaustive census. It distinguishes the manuscript actually reviewed from the raw file first uploaded before editorial screening. Published review histories generally sample accepted papers, not all submissions. A public review is not automatically a high-quality benchmark.

## Findings

| Venue | Public reports | Reviewed manuscript availability | Sampling implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| PeerJ | Mandatory for articles submitted from 13 February 2023; optional before that | Policy explicitly includes manuscript files and revised materials; version-labelled downloads | Direct pairing is feasible; older opt-in and accepted-paper selection must be recorded |
| PLOS ONE | Author opt-in at acceptance; scheme introduced 22 May 2019 | Policy lists decision letters, reviews, responses and attachments, not a guaranteed original-manuscript deposit | Large reports corpus; each paper needs separate source-version matching |
| PLOS Mental Health / Global Public Health | Check each journal's implementation of the PLOS scheme | Do not infer original-file availability from PLOS branding | Relevant expansion leads, not yet artifact-audited rows |
| The BMJ | Research and analysis papers generally publish prepublication history; research policy dates to September 2014 | Policy explicitly includes previous manuscript versions | Strong direct-pair source for behavioural/health-policy studies, with substantial domain selection |
| BMJ Open | Open reports and previous manuscript versions under journal policy | Draft-revision bundles are published alongside histories; exceptions exist | Direct-pair source, but medical/health scope differs from general social science |
| F1000Research | Signed reports published after article publication | Every article version is retained and citable; reviews refer to published versions | Direct version pairing; post-publication review is a separate evaluation stratum |
| Open Research Europe | Signed public reviews; versioned post-publication model | Versions linked and independently citable | Social-science coverage, but funding eligibility and publishing model select the population |
| eLife, current reviewed-preprint model | Public reviews and editorial assessment accompany reviewed preprints | Versioned reviewed preprints include the reviewed text; subsequent revised versions remain distinct | Useful social/behavioural work, but public assessments differ from conventional private reports |
| South African Journal of Science | Voluntary publication with author and reviewer consent; implementation from 2024 | An inspected 2025 review-history PDF appends the original manuscript | Genuine additional source, including qualitative social research; do not assume every consenting article has identical bundle contents |

## Policy evidence and artifact checks

**PeerJ.** The publisher's [review-history statement](https://peerj.com/articles/cs-254/reviews/) explicitly covers manuscript files, reviews, rebuttals and revisions, and specifies the February 2023 policy boundary. Our [PeerJ 236 history](https://peerj.com/articles/236/reviews/) supplies four Version 0.1 reports. The exact original manuscript was recovered from a public archive of the publisher's submission URL, with internal submission date and version checked. Provenance and SHA-256 are in `eval/corpus/empirical_pilot.v1.json`. This verifies availability, not review quality. The current paper remains a workflow diagnostic following user inspection of its weak human comparator set.

**PLOS ONE.** [Current editorial policy](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/editorial-and-peer-review-process) specifies optional publication at acceptance of decision letters, reports, author responses and their attachments. It does not promise original manuscript files. The [2024 publisher analysis](https://theplosblog.plos.org/2024/01/four-years-of-published-peer-review-history/) documents the May 2019 start and opt-in selection. [Feeling heard](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/peerReview?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0292865.r004) is an indexed social-relations review-history example; its original manuscript has **not** been verified here. An “Original Submission” date alone is insufficient. PLOS policy supports a reports corpus, not automatic version-matched admission.

**The BMJ.** [Publishing model](https://www.bmj.com/about-bmj/publishing-model?change_country=1) explicitly describes previous manuscript versions, reviewer comments, author responses and additional editorial materials. [Reviewer resources](https://www.bmj.com/content/resources-reviewers1-0) date the research-paper policy to submissions from September 2014. The policy says histories are usually posted and allows omissions/delays; confirm the actual file for each paper. No new BMJ article has been admitted to our corpus in this audit.

**BMJ Open.** [Author guidance](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/pages/authors) explicitly says previous versions, comments and replies are made available at publication. An indexed [draft-revision bundle for article e071701](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/bmjopen/13/7/e071701.draft-revisions.pdf) carries the publisher's explanation of this policy and exceptions. Direct PDF retrieval returned HTTP 403 in this environment, so this example is **policy/index verified, not locally downloaded or version matched**. Distinguish research articles from protocols and other article types when sampling.

**F1000Research.** [How it works](https://f1000research.com/about) states that reports are published against the current published article version, versions remain publicly accessible, and reports use CC BY. The [Social Psychology gateway](https://f1000research.com/gateways/social-psychology) provides an operational enumeration route; it includes completed research, protocols and reviews, some still awaiting reports. For example, DOI `10.12688/f1000research.179039.1` is a version-one loneliness/trustworthiness study with a reported review. This is an indexed candidate, not an admitted pair. The older [perception study](https://f1000research.com/articles/11-69/v2) shows revised-version presentation. Direct v1 retrieval was blocked here; verify article and report version identifiers when materializing. Exclude Faculty Reviews and unreviewed content from an ordinary empirical-paper stratum.

**Open Research Europe.** The [ERC introduction](https://erc.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-12/Open-Research-Europe-short-introduction-for-ERC-grantees.pdf) describes signed reports, author responses and independently citable linked versions, for research arising from eligible EU funding. This establishes the platform model; no individual ORE pair was downloaded in this audit. Check current funding eligibility and each report's version before enumeration. Do not pool it silently with conventional prepublication journal review.

**eLife.** The [current process](https://elifesciences.org/about/peer-review) defines a Reviewed Preprint as manuscript, assessment, public reviews and an optional response, with revised versions separately issued. The [2023 model announcement](https://elifesciences.org/for-the-press/b2329859/elife-ends-accept-reject-decisions-following-peer-review) distinguishes this from accept/reject decisions after review; selection still occurs before review. [Gender differences in submission behavior](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/90049v2/reviews) is an indexed social-science example exposing v1, v2, v3 and Version of Record history, with three public reviewers. Direct v1 PDF retrieval returned HTTP 403 here; no pair has been admitted. Separate individual public reviews from the joint editorial assessment and author responses. Check whether detailed recommendations are present in the specific version rather than assuming the public assessment is the entire reviewer communication.

**South African Journal of Science.** The managing editor's [2024 account](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?pid=S0038-23532024000600020&script=sci_arttext) dates implementation to 2024 and describes voluntary uptake. The [actual 2025 history PDF](https://sajs.co.za/article/download/19020/24594), DOI `10.17159/sajs.2025/19020/peerreview`, was downloaded and inspected: 24 pages; reviewer/response material on pages 1–5; original manuscript begins on PDF page 6, explicitly labelled “Original manuscript for review”. It concerns a qualitative study of community food practices. The manuscript's original title differs from the final title, illustrating why matching on title alone fails. SHA-256: `8c133e4debadf168eba46800df04d13f950354fc4d340a8e5fbc7b3ddf295765`. Raw PDF stays in ignored `eval/corpus/cache/journal-policy-examples/`. This confirms one available original, not uniform journal coverage; confirm bundle-specific reuse terms before redistribution.

## Search record and limits

Queries combined publisher domains with `peer review history`, `original manuscript`, `previous versions`, `version 1`, and social/behavioural topic terms. Current policy pages were preferred to third-party summaries. Article pages/bundles were checked to distinguish actual links from dated event labels. HTTP access failures are recorded separately from absence of public material. Examples verify availability and structure, not representativeness or review quality. No reviews or manuscripts were sent to model reviewers as part of this policy search.

Copernicus' [interactive-review policy](https://www.copernicus.org/news_and_press/2018-08-29_open-letter-on-the-publication-of-peer-review-reports.html) is a further cross-discipline route: public discussion manuscripts, invited reports, comments and versions. It applies to participating journals, not every Copernicus title. Geographical/social-environmental expansion requires a journal-specific check and is not counted as an admitted social-science source here.
