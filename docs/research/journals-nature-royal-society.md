# Open peer-review corpus audit: Nature Portfolio and Royal Society

Checked 2026-09-07. This is a source audit for constructing a validation sampling frame, not a claim that every article in a named journal has public reviews. **Policy** below means an official publisher statement. **Observed** means the contents of one downloaded article-level review file were inspected. A review file labelled “Version 0” is not the Version 0 manuscript: it labels the review round.

## Findings at a glance

## Nature Portfolio

Nature Portfolio's files are useful evidence about the *conversation* around an accepted paper. They are not, by themselves, the document the first-round reviewers read.

### Nature Human Behaviour

The journal's [transparent peer-review announcement](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-019-0799-8) says that, from December 2019, authors of research articles can opt to publish the full peer-review record: reviewer comments, editorial decision letters and author responses. It also warns that reviews inherited through transfers may not be included. The [signed-comments policy](https://www.nature.com/nathumbehav/editorial-policies/signed-comments) identifies the applicable cohort as research manuscripts submitted after 1 December 2019.

**Observed example.** Druckman et al., “Representation in science and trust in scientists in the USA,” DOI [10.1038/s41562-025-02358-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02358-4), links an official [Peer Review File](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41562-025-02358-4/MediaObjects/41562_2025_2358_MOESM3_ESM.pdf). Direct inspection found decision letters and reviewer material organized as Version 0 and Version 1. It did not contain a standalone submitted manuscript. The PDF itself states CC BY 4.0.

### Nature Communications

The journal's [transparent peer-review FAQ](https://www.nature.com/ncomms/submit/tpr-faq) states that peer-review files became mandatory for primary research papers submitted from 1 November 2022. From January 2016 to that date, authors chose whether to publish the file; the journal reports participation of about 70%. The promised contents are reviewer comments and author rebuttals. This scope is accepted/published primary research, so it does not guarantee public files for rejected submissions.

**Observed example.** “Satellite data uncover nationwide air pollution impacts from warehousing,” DOI [10.1038/s41467-024-50000-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50000-0), has an official [Peer Review File](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-024-50000-0/MediaObjects/41467_2024_50000_MOESM2_ESM.pdf). Direct inspection found three reviewers' comments across rounds and author material, but no submitted-manuscript PDF. The review file states CC BY 4.0.

### Communications Psychology

The current [editorial-process policy](https://www.nature.com/commspsychol/submit/editorial-process) says the journal publishes decision letters (including reviewer comments) and rebuttal letters for revised versions of published articles. It also says transferred records from the originating Nature Portfolio journal are not published, confidential material can be redacted, and the file need not contain everything considered by editors. The policy does not list submitted manuscript versions among the published components.

**Observed example.** Barry et al., “People overlook subtractive solutions to mental health problems,” DOI [10.1038/s44271-025-00312-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-025-00312-8), has an official [Peer Review File](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44271-025-00312-8/MediaObjects/44271_2025_312_MOESM1_ESM.pdf). Direct inspection found decision letters labelled Version 0–2, reviewer comments and ordered author rebuttals. It did not contain the Version 0 manuscript. The file states CC BY 4.0.

### Nature

Nature's [June 2025 editorial](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-01880-9) says the journal offered opt-in publication from 2020 and made referee reports and author responses automatic for new submissions from 16 June 2025 that are eventually published as research articles. Thus rejected-only submissions fall outside the stated publication guarantee.

**Observed example.** Lee and Sabatini, DOI [10.1038/s41586-021-04055-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04055-4), is an opt-in-era article with an official [Peer Review File](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-021-04055-4/MediaObjects/41586_2021_4055_MOESM1_ESM.pdf). Direct inspection found reports explicitly headed as applying to the initial version and author rebuttals, but no submitted-manuscript PDF. This example establishes the file pattern, not coverage under the later mandatory policy.

Across these four titles, an external timestamped preprint can only be a **high-confidence proxy** after comparison of title, authors, study count, tables, reported values and review-letter references. Those checks corroborate identity but do not prove it is the exact reviewed file. The strongest `exact` tier requires an author- or journal-deposited file explicitly identified as the reviewed version, or a byte/text match to a known submission. A round label or pre-review timestamp alone is insufficient; proxies must be analysed separately.

## Royal Society journals

The Royal Society's current [editorial standards](https://royalsociety.org/journals/ethics-policies/editorial-standards/) make publication of peer-review information mandatory for Royal Society Open Science, Open Biology and Proceedings B, and optional for Proceedings A and Biology Letters. Its [reviewer guidance](https://royalsociety.org/journals/reviewers/) describes transparent records and indicates that review material is shared under CC BY 4.0. The current pages do not give a reliable introduction date for every title, so dates must be established per article or via archived policy pages before longitudinal sampling.

**Observed example.** Kronmüller et al., “The positive side of a negative reference,” DOI [10.1098/rsos.160827](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.160827), has a preserved [review-history PDF](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/87903355/rsos160827_review_history.pdf). Inspection shows an original-submission date, an `R0 (Original submission)` review form, subsequent chronology, and a CC BY 4.0 notice. It does **not** reproduce the R0 manuscript. The label identifies the round reviewed, not a downloadable manuscript version. Current Royal Society article pages expose newer histories through a Web of Science transparent-peer-review endpoint; access and preservation should be tested during corpus acquisition.

Royal Society policies cover peer-review information for papers that are published. They do not guarantee a public archive of rejected-only manuscripts or reports. A transfer note may also indicate that some reviewing happened at another journal.

## Science Advances boundary check

Science Advances is relevant to broad social-science sampling, but the official AAAS material located in this audit described editorial peer review and open access to articles, not routine publication of referee reports, decisions, responses or submitted manuscripts. No article-level public review package was verified. This is an **unlocated workflow**, not evidence that no Science Advances article has ever disclosed reviews. It should remain outside the open-review population until primary-source evidence establishes a reproducible article cohort.

## Sampling rules

1. Sample only article types covered by the policy and submission-date cohort.
2. Save the official policy page and article page alongside the review file; policy pages change.
3. Inventory every component separately: reports, decisions, responses, manuscript files and rounds.
4. Hash the candidate manuscript and review files. Record how the manuscript was matched to round 0.
5. Treat rejected submissions as unavailable unless an official repository explicitly exposes them.
6. Keep `workflow guarantee`, `observed article example`, and `unlocated` as distinct evidence levels.
7. Do not train or generate the AI review with human reports in context; reveal them only during evaluation.
