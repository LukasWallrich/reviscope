# Public peer-review sources in psychology and adjacent social science

Checked 7 September 2026. This is a **bounded, purposive policy inventory**, not a world-complete census or a claim that every article or historical volume follows the publisher's current policy. It concentrates on plausible psychology and adjacent-social-science sources for a version-matched evaluation corpus. “Public review” means the report text or files are publicly accessible, not merely that reviewers may sign their names. “Exact manuscript” means the version evaluated in the corresponding review round can be obtained and linked with defensible version evidence.

## APS and SAGE

### Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science (AMPPS)

APS states that AMPPS is fully open access and uses **full Transparent Peer Review**, with a corresponding review record for each published manuscript on Web of Science. SAGE lists AMPPS among the selected journals in its ScholarOne Transparent Peer Review programme. SAGE describes those records as the full history for published articles: every review stage, author responses, and final editor decision letters, each with a DOI. Some participating journals permit author or reviewer opt-out, so item-level confirmation remains necessary. [APS journal page](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/ampps), [SAGE transparent-review policy](https://uk.sagepub.com/en-gb/eur/transparent-peer-review)

A concrete record exists for *An Inception-Cohort Study Quantifying How Many Registered Studies Are Publicly Shared*: Web of Science exposes two dated review rounds and a versioned review identifier. This establishes public reports and round structure. The record excerpt does not establish a downloadable byte-exact manuscript for each round, so that remains a case-level verification task. [Example review record](https://publons.com/wos-op/review/25076509/)

AMPPS is a valuable source for testing methodological review, but APS explicitly says its empirical scope emphasizes large-scale, methodological, metascience, and registered-report work rather than ordinary single-lab theoretical studies. It should not stand in for conventional empirical social psychology. [AMPPS submission guidance](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/ampps/ampps-submission-guidelines)

### Psychological Science and SPPS journals

Current Psychological Science guidance documents a three-tier editorial and external-review process, open-science requirements, and acceptance/rejection decisions. APS also documents data, materials, and preregistration badges. Those forms of research transparency do not themselves publish referee reports or the exact reviewed manuscript. [Psychological Science submissions](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/psychological_science/ps-submissions), [APS open-science policy](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/open-science)

SAGE's current list of transparent-review journals includes AMPPS, European Journal of Personality, and Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology, but not Psychological Science, Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, or Personality and Social Psychology Review. This supports “not listed in the current SAGE programme,” not a universal claim that no public review record can exist through an author archive or preprint. SAGE allows preprints subject to journal policy, but authors are asked not to post updated versions during review; therefore a preprint date alone does not prove exact review-round identity. [SAGE journal list](https://uk.sagepub.com/en-gb/eur/transparent-peer-review), [SAGE preprint policy](https://uk.sagepub.com/en-gb/afr/prior-publication)

**Rejected coverage:** SAGE's programme is described for articles published online. No systematic public set of rejected APS/SAGE submissions was established from these policies.

## APA journals

JPSP's official submission guidance requires substantial transparency for citations, data, code, materials, design, analysis, and preregistration. This is strong research transparency, but the guidance located here does not promise public reviewer reports, decision letters, or submitted-manuscript versions. An editorial encourages PsyArXiv posting, which can create usable manuscript snapshots only when dates, hashes, and content demonstrate that the snapshot is the reviewed version. [JPSP submission guidance](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/psp/submit), [JPSP editorial discussing PsyArXiv](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/features/psp-pspp0000403.pdf)

This scan did not establish a publisher-wide transparent-review corpus for JPSP or other APA journals. That is a statement about evidence found in current official guidance, not proof that isolated authors never release reports or submissions.

**Licensing and rejected coverage:** APA's submission pages do not grant a blanket licence for unpublished submissions or review reports. Any author-posted files need their own terms. No systematic rejected-paper corpus was established.

## Wiley, BPS, and adjacent psychology journals

Wiley describes Transparent Peer Review as an option at more than 90 participating journals: reviewer reports, author responses, and editor decision letters are published with accepted articles; authors can opt out and reviewers may remain anonymous. This policy does not promise publication of the original submitted manuscript. [Wiley policy infographic](https://authorservices.wiley.com/asset/Transparent%20Peer%20Review.pdf), [Wiley peer-review models](https://authorservices.wiley.com/asset/Types%20of%20Peer%20Review%20Infographic.pdf)

Concrete psychology examples include:

- *Socioeconomic status and access to psychotherapy*, **Journal of Clinical Psychology**, which links its public review history. [Article and review-history link](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jclp.23449)
- *Addressing Adverse Community Environments: Participant Recommendations to Foster Community Well-Being*, **Journal of Community Psychology**, which links a Web of Science peer-review record. [Article and review-history link](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jcop.23185)

These examples establish that psychology articles occur in Wiley's programme. They do not establish that the review platform exposes the original submitted manuscript. For each candidate, look for a dated preprint or archived submission and verify it against quotations, page references, round labels, and hashes.

The BPS/Wiley journal family requires title-by-title checking. Wiley's programme count and examples cannot be projected onto every BPS journal. No official evidence reviewed here establishes systematic publication of reports for British Journal of Social Psychology or the other BPS titles.

**Licensing and rejected coverage:** Article licences do not automatically license review-history files. Check the review record's own rights statement. Wiley describes publication alongside accepted articles; rejected reports are not a systematic public corpus under this model.

## PsychOpen GOLD

PsychOpen GOLD is a diamond-open platform for psychology. Its standards require at least two independent external experts but explicitly allow journals to use open, single-anonymized, or double-anonymized review. Its open-science standards support preprints and apply CC BY to published articles. They do not impose platform-wide publication of reviewer reports or original submitted manuscripts. [General publishing standards](https://psychopen.eu/policies/general-publishing-standards/), [Open-science standards](https://psychopen.eu/policies/open-science-standards/)

Accordingly, PsychOpen GOLD is a **journal-by-journal discovery source**, not a guaranteed open-review corpus. A public preprint can support exact-version matching only with round-specific evidence. The platform has announced that publishing services will end in 2027 while existing content remains accessible, which makes early capture and hashing prudent for any admitted record. [Platform notice](https://psychopen.eu/)

**Rejected coverage and review-file licensing:** unknown at platform level from the official policies reviewed. CC BY for published articles should not be generalized to unpublished review materials.

## Meta-Psychology

Meta-Psychology remains a practically strong source because its public OSF editorial archive has yielded separable submitted manuscripts, review files, and revisions with exact-version evidence in the current project. It is narrower and less prominent than the mainstream targets above. Its verified cases demonstrate feasibility, not representativeness.

The project should retain a record only when the archive names the initial submission and review round, quotations/page references agree with that file, and hashes are frozen. File-level redistribution terms should be checked independently of the published article's licence. Rejected-paper coverage has not been established as systematic.

## Collabra: Psychology

Collabra is a fully open-access psychology journal and the official journal of the Society for the Improvement of Psychological Science. In an official University of California Press interview dated 26 October 2020, editor-in-chief Simine Vazire states that the journal replaced its optional model: all published papers have reviews and decision letters published alongside them. The same announcement says authors receive permission to share reviews and decision letters even when a paper is rejected. A later primary interview confirms the journal's open-review practice. [UC Press policy announcement](https://www.ucpress.edu/blog-posts/52861-whats-new-with-collabra-psychology-a-qa-with-editor-in-chief-simine-vazire), [2021 editor interview](https://blog.scholasticahq.com/post/how-we-open-knowledge-vazire-collabra-psychology/)

This makes Collabra a strong discipline-fit discovery source. The journal page and examples inspected here did not establish a universal download of the original submitted manuscript. Article “received” dates and public final PDFs are insufficient for exact matching. Candidate records need a versioned preprint, archived submission, or another primary link tied to the review round.

**Licensing:** published Collabra articles are open access, commonly CC BY; verify the specific licence attached to reviews and decision files. **Rejected coverage:** author permission to disclose rejected reviews and letters makes individual author-released cases possible, but the policy does not establish a systematic journal archive of rejected submissions.

## BMC Psychology and BMC Psychiatry

BMC describes transparent peer review across its journal portfolio: for accepted papers, reviewer reports are published alongside the article, while reviewers may choose whether to sign. This makes BMC Psychology and BMC Psychiatry plausible sources of public human reports. [BMC portfolio account](https://blogs.biomedcentral.com/bmcblog/2022/05/12/why-choose-a-bmc-journal/), [BMC transition account](https://blogs.biomedcentral.com/bmcblog/2020/05/04/transparency-openness-and-peer-review/)

The manuscript-version history has a crucial date boundary. BMC announced that, from **30 March 2015**, it would stop routinely making earlier manuscript versions downloadable; readers could request them instead. A current BMC Psychology history can therefore label an entry “Original manuscript” without providing a download link. For example, the history for DOI `10.1186/s40359-021-00709-0` exposes reports and an “Original manuscript” timeline item, but the inspected page does not supply that manuscript file. [2015 BMC policy change](https://blogs.biomedcentral.com/bmcblog/2015/03/24/confidentiality-versus-transparency-publishers-dilemma/), [2021 BMC Psychology example](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40359-021-00709-0/peer-review)

Earlier records need separate treatment. At BMC Psychology's 2013 launch, BMC described publishing reports together with manuscript iterations. Pre-policy-change histories may therefore yield complete pairs, but their files and round alignment still need direct verification rather than extrapolation from the historical announcement. [2013 launch policy](https://blogs.biomedcentral.com/bmcseriesblog/2013/02/27/bmc-psychology-officially-launches/)

**Corpus consequence:** post-March-2015 BMC records should not be admitted as exact public pairs merely because the timeline contains an “Original manuscript” label. Email-on-request access is useful for negotiated research access, but it is not a reproducible public-download population. BMC's article licence also should not be presumed to govern referee reports or prior manuscript files. The located policy concerns accepted papers; it does not establish systematic rejected-paper coverage.

## Breadth beyond psychology

### SAGE: Research & Politics

Research & Politics is a conventional, fully open-access political-science journal with substantial data and analytic-material requirements. Its current submission guidance nevertheless states **“Review information published: None.”** It uses double-anonymized review, normally with two external reviewers, while public OA articles and replication materials are separate from referee reports. [Submission and peer-review policy](https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/rap), [transparency and replication policy](https://journals.sagepub.com/page/rap/transparency-openness-replication-policy)

Research & Politics is also absent from SAGE's current list of selected transparent-review journals. That is direct policy evidence that it is not a systematic source of public review reports under the present programme; it does not rule out an author independently archiving a submission and reviews. [SAGE transparent-review list](https://uk.sagepub.com/en-gb/eur/transparent-peer-review)

SAGE's listed programme does provide some adjacent breadth. **European Journal of Personality** and **Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology** are empirical psychology candidates, while **Planning Theory** broadens subject matter but is unlikely to supply ordinary empirical social-science cases. Participating records can include reports, responses, decisions, and stages, but the programme does not promise downloadable reviewed manuscripts and permits opt-out.

### Sociological Science

Sociological Science is a general-interest, CC BY open-access sociology journal spanning experiments, ethnography, computational work, and quantitative analysis. Its editorial model is deliberately evaluative and fast: editors decide, may consult specialists, and do not use conventional revise-and-resubmit cycles. [Journal information](https://sociologicalscience.com/about/journal-information/), [editorial and review process](https://sociologicalscience.com/for-authors/editorial-and-review-process/), [submission scope](https://sociologicalscience.com/for-authors/submission-guidelines/)

The official pages inspected describe the internal process and encourage public post-publication debate. They do **not** promise publication of consulting-editor assessments, decision letters, or the submitted manuscript. Open access and public commentary therefore do not make this a version-matched human-review corpus. Isolated author-released cases remain possible and would need independent version evidence and permissions.

### Education: Frontiers in Education and AERA Open

Frontiers in Education calls its process transparent and publishes the names of endorsing reviewers and the handling editor with accepted articles. Its editor guidance is more specific about report availability: a reviewer receives a copy, but “the report itself is not to be shared in any public forum.” The public naming of reviewers must therefore not be coded as public report text. [Journal review overview](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/about), [editor guidance](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/for-authors/editor-guidelines)

Rejected or withdrawn reviewers are not named publicly, and the located policy does not promise original manuscript versions. Frontiers in Education is consequently unsuitable for direct report-text comparison under its documented default, despite its useful disciplinary scope.

AERA Open's Registered Reports programme creates stage-specific reviewed protocols and final reports, making it a potential education-design stratum. The official material located here explains the two-stage process but does not establish that the referee reports and exact stage-one submission are publicly downloadable. It remains a discovery lead rather than an admitted corpus. [AERA Open Registered Reports collection](https://journals.sagepub.com/page/ero/registered-reports)

### Management journals

This bounded scan did not locate an official publisher policy establishing public report text plus exact submitted versions for a conventional management journal. A scholarly argument for open peer review in management is not evidence that the journal publishing that argument follows such a policy. Management remains a deliberate gap for the next inventory pass; candidate titles should be assessed from their current author instructions and article-level records rather than from labels such as “anonymous,” “open access,” or “transparent.”

## Corpus construction implications

1. Separate three properties that publisher pages often blend: an open final article, public reports, and a public exact reviewed manuscript.
2. Admit human-comparison pairs only after review-round identity is established. A final paper plus reviews of an earlier version is ineligible.
3. Preserve each individual reviewer report. Do not substitute an editor letter or combined response for multiple human comparators.
4. Record report-level licences. The article's CC licence does not automatically cover editorial correspondence or an unpublished manuscript.
5. Treat accepted-paper-only programmes as selection-conditioned corpora. They cannot answer questions about rejected manuscripts or the full submission population.
6. Use rejected papers only where the journal or authors release both submission and reports with permission and reliable version evidence.
7. Keep journal prominence, discipline fit, and archive completeness as separate sampling dimensions. No single source identified here optimizes all three.
