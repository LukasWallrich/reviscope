## Answer

**ReviScope should be evaluated through human-adjudicated criticisms, paired comparisons across papers, and explicit cost controls.** Report-level LLM preferences are useful secondary outcomes, but they do not establish scientific correctness. The literature supports testing specialist decomposition and verification empirically; it does not establish that additional stages necessarily improve either coverage or precision.

This focused search includes **35 works whose linked sources were opened**, prioritising 2023–2026. Older sources address statistical questions that recent LLM papers often handle inadequately. Published papers, accepted manuscripts, and preprints are identified separately. Recommendations below are methodological proposals for ReviScope; proposed pilot sizes are not literature-established minimums.

### (a) Fifteen key design recommendations

- **Freeze an independently audited benchmark before comparing systems.** Separate demonstrable errors, reporting omissions, externally verifiable concerns, and invalid targets. Analyse these strata separately, with demonstrable errors as the clearest recall endpoint. Retain development papers for development and reserve untouched papers for the final comparison. Criticism extraction and matching themselves require validation: Liang and colleagues found appreciably imperfect matching despite excellent extraction performance. [Liang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01783v1)

- **Use several distinct outcomes instead of one “review quality” score.** Report audited-error recall; supported and contradicted criticisms; important supported findings per paper; unresolved concerns; and actionability. Evaluate theoretical, interpretive, and presentation contributions separately from error detection. A review can be useful without overlapping extensively with other reviewers, and a polished review can receive inflated substantive-quality scores. [Liang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01783), [Amirshahi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23264)

- **Represent criticisms as contextualised issue records.** Preserve the relevant study, passage, variable, inferential claim, alleged problem, rationale, and remedy; split independently assessable allegations. Cluster equivalent underlying problems while retaining their original wording. For equivalence, check bidirectional entailment; for benchmark detection, use directional coverage of the specific target. Audit false matches, missed paraphrases, over-splitting, and over-merging, including transitive clustering errors. Atomicity must preserve qualifications and context. [Gunjal and Durrett](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.215/), [Zha et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.634/)

- **Estimate precision by adjudicating emitted findings, including unmatched findings.** Absence from a planted key or human review does not establish invalidity. Pool findings across systems and humans, adjudicate the resulting issue clusters, and retain supported/contradicted/unresolved labels. Report the unresolved fraction and, where useful, bounds between `supported/all` and `(supported + unresolved)/all`; these are bounds on validity, not interchangeable precision estimates. Report empty outputs explicitly. [Min et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251), [Liang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01783)

- **Make expert adjudication a probability sample with known inclusion probabilities.** Oversample disputed matches, serious accusations, novel findings, verifier rejections, and judge disagreements, while giving every relevant stratum a positive selection probability. Record selection probabilities and use inverse-probability weighting for population totals or proportions; do not report an enriched disagreement sample as representative accuracy. Blind adjudicators to system identity and obtain independent ratings before resolution. [Angelopoulos et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09633), [Kim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16354)

- **Judge factual support, inference, and remedy separately using anchored checklists.** Ask whether the manuscript supports the factual premise, whether the criticism follows, whether it matters, and whether the proposed remedy is necessary and proportionate. Supply manuscript evidence and expert-approved examples where available. Missing retrieved evidence should trigger further inspection or abstention, especially for absence claims. Checklist and reference-guided judging are promising, but require domain validation. [Lee et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.796/), [Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08491)

- **Test presentation invariance, alongside order swaps and ties.** Run pairwise comparisons in both orders and report reversal rates separately from substantive ties or insufficient evidence. Probe faithful changes to length, organisation, confidence, formatting, and rhetorical polish. Compare full reports and standardised issue presentations. Length adjustment is a sensitivity analysis: length may also carry useful additional content, so controlling it changes the estimand. [Shi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791), [Dubois et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475), [Amirshahi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23264)

- **Use multiple judge families, but validate the panel against humans.** Preserve each judge’s verdict and examine generator-family × judge-family effects. A panel of smaller, diverse judges can outperform one expensive judge, but cross-family membership does not guarantee independent errors. Predefine aggregation and abstention rules; do not count the two answer orders as two independent judges. [Panickssery et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076), [Verga et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796)

- **Build a verifier probe set that distinguishes competence from persuasion.** Include supported, contradicted, unresolved, partially correct, and unnecessarily strong criticisms, with misleading rationales and excessive remedies. Vary rationale visibility, confidence language, and purported authority while holding the criticism constant. Measure confusion matrices, Brier scores, reliability plots, and precision–coverage curves; calibrate on held-out examples. Keep a representative sample alongside the deliberately difficult probe set. [Hwang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07805), [Sharma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548), [Tian et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975)

- **Compare practical performance and matched-budget performance separately.** Include the required one-call baseline, an improved one-call prompt, and a strong single-agent workflow with comparable evidence/tools. Share deterministic screening or study-map inputs where the question concerns specialist reasoning specifically. Compare configurations at several dollar budgets and report actual input, output, reasoning tokens, retrieval/tool calls, latency, and failures. Token matching alone does not equalise costs across models. [Wang et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.331/), [Tran and Kiela](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02460v1)

- **Measure what each stage adds, removes, and distorts.** Prioritise ablations of specialist decomposition, blind-spot passes, quote anchoring, verification, and editorial disposition. For a fixed candidate set, measure supported findings retained, contradicted findings rejected, and valid findings incorrectly rejected. Separately measure end-to-end performance after rerunning generation. Add verification to the baseline as well as removing it from ReviScope, so verification and specialist effects can be distinguished. [Dhuliawala et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11495v2), [Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01798v2), [Cemri et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/b1041e52d3be19f0a9bc491657488e4a-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html)

- **Pair configurations within papers and repeat generation.** Run every configuration on the same submitted manuscript versions, randomise execution order, and record model snapshots, prompts, evidence access, and decoding settings. As a proposed pilot, start with three generation runs per paper/configuration, then allocate further repeats according to observed variance. Repeated generations improve estimates for those papers; they do not substitute for additional papers. [Alvarado Gonzalez et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24086), [Westfall et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25111580/)

- **Make papers the primary sampling unit for inference.** Compute each paper’s configuration difference after averaging over runs, then summarise those paired differences. Bootstrap papers while retaining their associated findings, runs, and judgments; consider within-paper resampling when estimating generation variability too. Use hierarchical models as secondary analyses, with appropriate paper, issue, run, and rater structure. With very few papers, bootstrap intervals and complex mixed models remain fragile; show paper-level results and sensitivity analyses. [Dror et al.](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128/), [Bowyer et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/bowyer25a.html)

- **Plan rater and item counts through precision targets and pilot simulation.** A proposed rubric pilot is 50–100 issue clusters across several papers, with two independent experts and a third resolving disagreements; a random subset should receive three independent ratings. Estimate disagreement, prevalence, paper clustering, and run variance, then simulate the intended design. Report nominal Krippendorff’s α for support labels, ordinal α for ordered consequences, raw agreement, and AC1 as a sensitivity measure. No universal minimum establishes reliability. [Hayes and Krippendorff](https://chukharev.github.io/520/Hayes_Krippendorff_2007.pdf), [Vach and Gerke](https://findresearcher.sdu.dk/ws/files/232856519/1_s2.0_S2215016123002108_main.pdf), [Westfall et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25111580/)

- **Preregister what would make the added stages worthwhile.** Specify a primary coverage or important-supported-findings endpoint, a supported-precision non-inferiority margin, acceptable serious false-positive burden, and cost limits. Choose margins from editorial consequences before examining final comparisons. Require the appropriate confidence bound to clear each criterion; nonsignificance does not establish equivalence or non-inferiority. For actionability, experts assess remedy necessity while a crowd exercise measures comprehension and usability. [Lakens](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/80918653/lakeequi2017.pdf)

### (b) Annotated bibliography

#### Judge reliability, bias, calibration, and mitigation

**1. Zheng, Lianmin, Wei-Lin Chiang, Ying Sheng, Siyuan Zhuang, Zhanghao Wu, Yonghao Zhuang, Zi Lin, Zhuohan Li, Dacheng Li, Eric P. Xing, Hao Zhang, Joseph E. Gonzalez, and Ion Stoica. (2023). [Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685). NeurIPS 2023, Datasets and Benchmarks Track.**

The authors compare LLM judgments with controlled expert and crowdsourced preferences and investigate position, verbosity, self-enhancement, and reasoning limitations. GPT-4 achieves over 80% agreement with human preferences in their settings. They examine mitigations including answer-order changes and reference-guided evaluation. This establishes feasibility for conversational preference evaluation, but its agreement figures should not be transferred to judging scientific criticism.

**2. Shi, Lin, Chiyu Ma, Wenhua Liang, Xingjian Diao, Weicheng Ma, and Soroush Vosoughi. (2025). [Judging the Judges: A Systematic Study of Position Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791). AACL-IJCNLP 2025; initially released as a 2024 preprint.**

The updated study evaluates 15 judges across 22 tasks and approximately 40 candidate models, producing over 150,000 evaluations. It separates repetition stability, position consistency, and preference fairness. Position bias varies across judges and tasks and depends strongly on the quality gap between candidates. ReviScope should therefore report order sensitivity directly, particularly when competing reports are closely matched.

**3. Dubois, Yann, Balázs Galambosi, Percy Liang, and Tatsunori B. Hashimoto. (2024). [Length-Controlled AlpacaEval: A Simple Way to Debias Automatic Evaluators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475). COLM 2024.**

The method fits a generalised linear model predicting pairwise preference and estimates the counterfactual preference at equal response lengths. Length control increases Spearman correlation with Chatbot Arena from 0.94 to 0.98 and reduces vulnerability to verbosity manipulation. It provides a useful sensitivity analysis for ReviScope’s report preferences. Its transfer requires care because additional review length can reflect additional valid findings, rather than presentation alone.

**4. Panickssery, Arjun, Samuel R. Bowman, and Shi Feng. (2024). [LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076). arXiv:2404.13076.**

The authors study self-recognition and self-preference in summarisation, including controlled fine-tuning interventions. GPT-4 and Llama 2 can recognise their outputs above trivial performance, and stronger recognition is associated with stronger self-preference. Controlled experiments support a causal relationship beyond straightforward confounders. Blinding model names is consequently insufficient, although the study does not establish the magnitude of bias for manuscript criticisms.

**5. Sharma, Mrinank, Meg Tong, Tomasz Korbak, David Duvenaud, Amanda Askell, Samuel R. Bowman, Newton Cheng, Esin Durmus, Zac Hatfield-Dodds, Scott R. Johnston, Shauna Kravec, Timothy Maxwell, Sam McCandlish, Kamal Ndousse, Oliver Rausch, Nicholas Schiefer, Da Yan, Miranda Zhang, and Ethan Perez. (2023). [Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548). arXiv:2310.13548; updated 2025.**

Five assistants exhibit sycophancy across four free-form tasks, including changing assessments in response to users’ expressed views. Human preference data and preference-model experiments suggest that convincing agreement can be rewarded over correctness. This supports testing whether a verifier accepts a criticism more readily when the prompt implies that an expert already endorses it. The study concerns general assistant behaviour rather than a dedicated scientific-review verifier.

**6. Hwang, Yerin, Dongryeol Lee, Taegwan Kang, Yongil Kim, and Kyomin Jung. (2025). [Can You Trick the Grader? Adversarial Persuasion of LLM Judges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07805). arXiv:2508.07805.**

The authors embed seven persuasion techniques into otherwise identical mathematical responses. Across six benchmarks, persuasive additions inflate scores for incorrect solutions by up to 8% on average, as reported by the authors; larger models and counter-prompts do not eliminate the effect. Pairwise judging is also susceptible. For ReviScope, this motivates separating evidence-based verification from the persuasiveness of a supplied rationale.

**7. Verga, Pat, Sebastian Hofstatter, Sophia Althammer, Yixuan Su, Aleksandra Piktus, Arkady Arkhangorodsky, Minjie Xu, Naomi White, and Patrick Lewis. (2024). [Replacing Judges with Juries: Evaluating LLM Generations with a Panel of Diverse Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796). arXiv:2404.18796.**

PoLL combines smaller judges from different model families across three judging settings and six datasets. On Natural Questions, its Cohen’s κ is 0.763 versus 0.627 for the single GPT-4 judge, and the authors report costs over seven times lower. Benefits are not universal: individual judges outperform the panel on some datasets, and prompt changes alter comparisons. This supports testing a diverse panel, with retained individual verdicts and human validation.

**8. Lee, Yukyung, JoongHoon Kim, Jaehee Kim, Hyowon Cho, Jaewook Kang, Pilsung Kang, and Najoung Kim. (2025). [CheckEval: A reliable LLM-as-a-Judge framework for evaluating text generation using checklists](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.796/). Proceedings of EMNLP 2025, 15771–15798.**

CheckEval replaces broad Likert judgments with decomposed binary checklist questions and evaluates 12 models. The paper reports an average inter-model agreement improvement of 0.45; large-model groups reach α and κ of 0.67 on SummEval and Topical-Chat. It also checks checklist quality and correspondence with human scores. This is strong methodological support for traceable judging, but shared agreement does not by itself establish scientific validity.

**9. Kim, Seungone, Jamin Shin, Yejin Cho, Joel Jang, Shayne Longpre, Hwaran Lee, Sangdoo Yun, Seongjin Shin, Sungdong Kim, James Thorne, and Minjoon Seo. (2024). [Prometheus: Inducing Fine-grained Evaluation Capability in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08491). ICLR 2024.**

Prometheus trains a 13B evaluator using customised rubrics, reference answers, and GPT-4-generated feedback. Across 45 customised rubrics, reported Pearson correlation with human evaluators is 0.897, compared with 0.882 for GPT-4 and 0.392 for ChatGPT. It demonstrates the potential of reference-guided, criterion-specific evaluation. The training provenance and task selection limit claims of independent human alignment or transfer to scientific fault-finding.

**10. Tian, Katherine, Eric Mitchell, Allan Zhou, Archit Sharma, Rafael Rafailov, Huaxiu Yao, Chelsea Finn, and Christopher D. Manning. (2023). [Just Ask for Calibration: Strategies for Eliciting Calibrated Confidence Scores from Language Models Fine-Tuned with Human Feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975). EMNLP 2023.**

The authors compare methods for eliciting confidence from RLHF models on TriviaQA, SciQ, and TruthfulQA. Verbalised confidence is often better calibrated than conditional token probabilities, with relative expected-calibration-error reductions around 50% in reported comparisons. This supports eliciting verifier confidence and evaluating its usefulness for deferral. It does not establish that confidence elicited for scientific criticism is calibrated without separate testing.

**11. Tan, Sijun, Siyuan Zhuang, Kyle Montgomery, William Y. Tang, Alejandro Cuadron, Chenguang Wang, Raluca Ada Popa, and Ion Stoica. (2025). [JudgeBench: A Benchmark for Evaluating LLM-based Judges](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12784v2). ICLR 2025.**

JudgeBench constructs challenging pairs containing objectively correct and incorrect responses across knowledge, reasoning, mathematics, and coding. On its main benchmark, vanilla GPT-4o judging achieves 50.86%, Arena-Hard prompting achieves 56.57%, and ChatEval achieves 34.00%. It tests whether verification is easier than solving and documents generation-model selection effects. ReviScope needs similarly difficult verifier probes; conversational preference agreement can conceal weak discrimination of correctness.

#### Evidence specifically concerning scientific reviews

**12. Liang, Weixin, Yuhui Zhang, Hancheng Cao, Binglu Wang, Daisy Ding, Xinyu Yang, Kailas Vodrahalli, Siyu He, Daniel Smith, Yian Yin, Daniel McFarland, and James Zou. (2023). [Can large language models provide useful feedback on research papers? A large-scale empirical analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01783v1). arXiv:2310.01783.**

The study compares GPT-4 feedback with reviews of 3,096 Nature-family papers and 1,709 ICLR papers. GPT–human criticism overlap is 30.85% and 39.23%, respectively, versus human–human overlap of 28.58% and 35.25%. Against human annotations, criticism matching achieves precision 0.777 and recall 0.878; 57.4% of surveyed researchers find generated feedback helpful or very helpful. This directly informs clustering and matcher audits, but overlap and perceived usefulness do not establish correctness.

**13. Du, Jiangshu, Yibo Wang, Wenting Zhao, Zhongfen Deng, Shuaiqi Liu, Renze Lou, Henry Peng Zou, Pranav Narayanan Venkit, Nan Zhang, Mukund Srinath, Ranran Haoran Zhang, Vipul Gupta, Yinghui Li, Tao Li, Fei Wang, Qin Liu, Tianlin Liu, Pengzhi Gao, Congying Xia, Chen Xing, Cheng Jiayang, Zhaowei Wang, Ying Su, Raj Sanjay Shah, Ruohao Guo, Jing Gu, Haoran Li, Kangda Wei, Zihao Wang, Lu Cheng, Surangika Ranathunga, Meng Fang, Jie Fu, Fei Liu, Ruihong Huang, Eduardo Blanco, Yixin Cao, Rui Zhang, Philip S. Yu, and Wenpeng Yin. (2024). [LLMs Assist NLP Researchers: Critique Paper (Meta-)Reviewing](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.292.pdf). Proceedings of EMNLP 2024, 5081–5099.**

ReviewCritique contains submitted manuscripts and expert-labelled deficient review segments. The best reported paper-averaged deficiency-detection F1 is 21.99 for Claude Opus; its corresponding precision is 16.94 and recall 42.12. This directly demonstrates over-flagging in scientific metareviewing. Its task and models differ from ReviScope, so the rates are diagnostic evidence rather than expected performance estimates.

**14. Ebrahimi, Sajad, Soroush Sadeghian, Ali Ghorbanpour, Negar Arabzadeh, Sara Salamat, Seyed Mohammad Hosseini, Hai Son Le, Mahdi Bashari, and Ebrahim Bagheri. (2026). [PeeriScope: A Multi-Faceted Framework for Evaluating Peer Review Quality](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24071v1). Companion Proceedings of the ACM Web Conference 2026.**

PeeriScope evaluates 753 reviews from 200 papers using structured features, rubric-guided LLM scores, and supervised estimators. GPT-4o’s Kendall’s τ is 0.359 for overall quality, 0.411 for actionability, and only 0.115 for factuality; structured-feature regressors outperform the zero-shot judges. Ratings use reviews with manuscript titles and abstracts, and annotators are trained graduate students. Its limited manuscript context particularly constrains interpretation of factuality performance.

**15. Amirshahi, Shakiba, Sajad Ebrahimi, Hai Son Le, Negar Arabzadeh, and Ebrahim Bagheri. (2026). [Judging a Review by its Cover: A Reliability Analysis of LLM-based Peer Review Evaluation Metrics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23264). Accepted for CIKM 2026; arXiv:2609.23264.**

The authors evaluate 29 content-oriented metrics using 4,044 faithful rewrites of 674 human reviews from ICLR and NeurIPS. Twenty-three metrics show significant score shifts, while six satisfy their robustness criterion, with broadly similar patterns across two judges. They distinguish sensitivity testing from equivalence testing. This directly supports ReviScope’s presentation-invariance probes, although rewrite fidelity and the chosen equivalence margins remain substantive assumptions.

#### Atomic evaluation, entailment, and verifiers

**16. Min, Sewon, Kalpesh Krishna, Xinxi Lyu, Mike Lewis, Wen-tau Yih, Pang Wei Koh, Mohit Iyyer, Luke Zettlemoyer, and Hannaneh Hajishirzi. (2023). [FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14251v2). Proceedings of EMNLP 2023, 12076–12100.**

FActScore decomposes text into atomic facts and measures the fraction supported by a designated knowledge source. ChatGPT biographies receive approximately 58% factual precision, while the best automated estimator approximates aggregate scores with reported error below 2%. That aggregate error does not mean fewer than 2% of individual claims are misclassified. Its precision framework is useful for ReviScope, but scientific criticisms additionally require judgments about inference, importance, and remedies.

**17. Wei, Jerry, Chengrun Yang, Xinying Song, Yifeng Lu, Nathan Hu, Jie Huang, Dustin Tran, Daiyi Peng, Ruibo Liu, Da Huang, Cosmo Du, and Quoc V. Le. (2024). [Long-form factuality in large language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802). NeurIPS 2024.**

SAFE decomposes responses and uses iterative web search to assess individual facts. Across roughly 16,000 facts, it agrees with crowd annotations 72% of the time; further adjudication favours SAFE in 76% of 100 sampled disagreements. Reported annotation cost is over twenty times lower than human evaluation. Its “recall” component depends on a preferred-length parameter, so it should not be interpreted as recall of all scientific problems in a manuscript.

**18. Gunjal, Anisha, and Greg Durrett. (2024). [Molecular Facts: Desiderata for Decontextualization in LLM Fact Verification](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.215/). Findings of EMNLP 2024, 3751–3768.**

The authors balance standalone interpretability against adding unnecessary information when decomposing claims. In a small human analysis of 25 claims, molecular decontextualisation produces 24% ambiguous claims, versus 76% for the SAFE baseline; broader experiments examine 726 verification claims. The paper also documents erroneous entailment judgments and wrong-entity evidence matches. ReviScope should retain study-specific context and audit decomposition, rather than assuming smaller claim units are always safer.

**19. Zha, Yuheng, Yichi Yang, Ruichen Li, and Zhiting Hu. (2023). [AlignScore: Evaluating Factual Consistency with A Unified Alignment Function](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.634/). Proceedings of ACL 2023, 11328–11348.**

AlignScore trains a unified alignment function using 4.7 million examples from seven tasks, including NLI, fact verification, and semantic similarity. Evaluation covers 22 datasets, 19 unseen during training, and the 355M-parameter model matches or exceeds substantially larger LLM metrics in reported comparisons. It offers a comparatively inexpensive alternative or complement to LLM matching. Alignment scores require local calibration and cannot establish the methodological validity of a criticism.

**20. Saad-Falcon, Jon, E. Kelly Buchanan, Mayee F. Chen, Tzu-Heng Huang, Brendan McLaughlin, Tanvir Bhathal, Shang Zhu, Ben Athiwaratkun, Frederic Sala, Scott Linderman, Azalia Mirhoseini, and Christopher Ré. (2025). [Shrinking the Generation-Verification Gap with Weak Verifiers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18203). NeurIPS 2025.**

Weaver combines imperfect verifiers using weak supervision, estimated accuracies, output normalisation, and filtering. With Llama 3.3 70B generating candidates, the reported selected-answer average is 87.7% across the studied reasoning and mathematics tasks. Weighted combinations outperform unweighted combinations because verifier competence differs. This motivates calibrated aggregation for ReviScope, but candidate selection with objective answers differs from accepting open-ended scientific criticism.

#### Self-correction, staged systems, and multi-agent comparisons

**21. Huang, Jie, Xinyun Chen, Swaroop Mishra, Huaixiu Steven Zheng, Adams Wei Yu, Xinying Song, and Denny Zhou. (2024). [Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01798v2). ICLR 2024.**

The study distinguishes intrinsic correction from correction aided by oracle correctness labels and examines baseline fairness. On GSM8K, GPT-4 accuracy falls from 95.5% initially to 91.5% and 89.0% after successive intrinsic-correction rounds. Correct answers can be changed into incorrect ones, and apparent gains can depend on privileged feedback or weaker prompts. This supports auditing harmful verifier/editorial changes, while leaving open improvements from external evidence and newer models.

**22. Dhuliawala, Shehzaad, Mojtaba Komeili, Jing Xu, Roberta Raileanu, Xian Li, Asli Celikyilmaz, and Jason Weston. (2023). [Chain-of-Verification Reduces Hallucination in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11495v2). arXiv:2309.11495.**

CoVe drafts an answer, generates checking questions, answers them independently, and revises the draft. Biography FActScore rises from 55.9 to 71.4, while average facts fall from 16.6 to 12.3; MultiSpanQA F1 improves from 0.39 to 0.48. The independent checking context helps avoid copying initial hallucinations. This supplies positive evidence for staging, but also demonstrates why ReviScope must measure retained coverage alongside precision gains.

**23. Du, Yilun, Shuang Li, Antonio Torralba, Joshua B. Tenenbaum, and Igor Mordatch. (2023). [Improving Factuality and Reasoning in Language Models through Multiagent Debate](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14325v1). arXiv:2305.14325.**

Three agents exchange proposed solutions over two debate rounds in the reported comparison. Arithmetic accuracy improves from 67.0% for a single agent to 81.8% for debate, and GSM8K improves from 77.0% to 85.0%, with a majority baseline at 81.0%. The work also studies factuality. These are useful positive results, but they do not establish superiority over a strong single agent given the same total computation.

**24. Wang, Qineng, Zihao Wang, Ying Su, Hanghang Tong, and Yangqiu Song. (2024). [Rethinking the Bounds of LLM Reasoning: Are Multi-Agent Discussions the Key?](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.331/). Proceedings of ACL 2024, 6106–6131.**

The authors systematically compare discussion mechanisms and single-agent prompting across reasoning tasks and models. Strong single-agent prompts attain approximately the best discussion performance, and the reported discussion advantage appears when demonstrations are absent. The result highlights prompt quality as a confound in architecture comparisons. ReviScope’s one-call baseline should receive comparable prompt-development attention, although this study does not test scientific-review specialists.

**25. Cemri, Mert, Melissa Z. Pan, Shuyi Yang, Lakshya A. Agrawal, Bhavya Chopra, Rishabh Tiwari, Kurt Keutzer, Aditya Parameswaran, Dan Klein, Kannan Ramchandran, Matei A. Zaharia, Joseph E. Gonzalez, and Ion Stoica. (2025). [Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/b1041e52d3be19f0a9bc491657488e4a-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html). NeurIPS 2025, Datasets and Benchmarks Track.**

MAST organises 14 failure modes into system-design problems, inter-agent misalignment, and task verification. The final dataset contains over 1,600 traces from seven frameworks, with taxonomy development using 150 traces and reported annotator κ of 0.88. It provides a framework for coding information loss, role problems, and verification failures in ReviScope traces. This is a failure-analysis resource, not a universal causal estimate of the effect of adding agents.

**26. Tran, Dat, and Douwe Kiela. (2026). [Single-Agent LLMs Outperform Multi-Agent Systems on Multi-Hop Reasoning Under Equal Thinking Token Budgets](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02460v1). arXiv:2604.02460.**

The study compares single agents with sequential, subtask, role, debate, and ensemble architectures across three model families. At a nominal 1,000-thinking-token budget, Table 1’s average accuracy is 0.418 for the standard single agent versus 0.388 for debate and 0.379 for sequential agents. There are low-budget and model/task exceptions, and API budget controls create complications. The information-theoretic argument assumes perfect context utilisation; neither it nor these results proves that specialists cannot help manuscript review.

#### Evaluation design, inference, sampling, and reliability

**27. Dror, Rotem, Gili Baumer, Segev Shlomov, and Roi Reichart. (2018). [The Hitchhiker’s Guide to Testing Statistical Significance in Natural Language Processing](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128/). Proceedings of ACL 2018, 1383–1392.**

This methodological paper matches significance tests to evaluation metrics and experimental designs, discussing paired bootstrap and randomisation approaches. Its survey identifies ignored or misused significance testing in NLP research. It supports comparing systems on shared evaluation units and preserving pairing during resampling. For ReviScope, applying these principles at the paper level is a design adaptation; the paper does not solve very-small-cluster inference automatically.

**28. Bowyer, Sam, Laurence Aitchison, and Desi R. Ivanova. (2025). [Position: Don’t Use the CLT in LLM Evals With Fewer Than a Few Hundred Datapoints](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/bowyer25a.html). Proceedings of ICML 2025, PMLR 267, 81143–81184.**

The authors show that central-limit-theorem uncertainty calculations can substantially underestimate uncertainty in small, specialised LLM evaluations. They recommend alternative frequentist and Bayesian approaches. This supports avoiding routine normal-approximation error bars for ReviScope’s small benchmark. The title’s scale is not a sample-size guarantee, and its arguments must be adapted to dependent criticisms clustered within papers.

**29. Alvarado Gonzalez, Miguel Angel, Michelle Bruno Hernandez, Miguel Angel Peñaloza Perez, Bruno Lopez Orozco, Jesus Tadeo Cruz Soto, and Sandra Malagon. (2025). [Do Repetitions Matter? Strengthening Reliability in LLM Evaluations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24086). arXiv:2509.24086.**

The authors evaluate eight models with three runs per setting and use mixed-effects logistic regression and rank-instability analyses. Ten of twelve benchmark slices contain a single-run pairwise ranking inversion relative to the three-run majority; two runs remove approximately 83% of these inversions. Standard-error reductions are much smaller than the ranking gains. This supports repeated generation, but its repetition recommendations are specific to the studied mathematical benchmark.

**30. Angelopoulos, Anastasios N., Stephen Bates, Clara Fannjiang, Michael I. Jordan, and Tijana Zrnic. (2023). [Prediction-Powered Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09633). Science, 382(6671), 669–674.**

Prediction-powered inference combines abundant machine predictions with a smaller labelled sample and corrects prediction errors when estimating population quantities. The framework supplies confidence intervals for means, quantiles, and regression parameters without assuming the prediction model is correct. Better predictions can increase statistical efficiency, but there is no universal annotation-saving percentage. It offers a principled role for ReviScope judges as auxiliary measurements, subject to sampling and dependence assumptions.

**31. Kim, Jane Paik. (2026). [Augmenting Human Evaluation with LLM Judges: How Many Human Reviews Do You Need?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16354). arXiv:2605.16354.**

This preprint formulates LLM-assisted evaluation as a two-stage sampling design: machine judgments for all observations and human ratings for a subsample. It proposes a doubly robust estimator using known sampling probabilities and derives asymptotic variance for sample-size and allocation planning. More human effort can be allocated where machine ratings predict human ratings poorly. It closely matches ReviScope’s adjudication plan, but asymptotic results need scrutiny with few papers or extreme weights.

**32. Westfall, Jacob, David A. Kenny, and Charles M. Judd. (2014). [Statistical power and optimal design in experiments in which samples of participants respond to samples of stimuli](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25111580/). Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 143(5), 2020–2045.**

The paper derives power calculations for five designs involving participant and stimulus sampling. It shows that adding participants alone can leave power bounded below one when the stimulus sample remains small. For ReviScope, papers resemble sampled stimuli, while raters and repeated outputs introduce further variation. The exact mapping requires adaptation, but the central implication is direct: more judgments of the same few papers cannot establish broad generalisation.

**33. Lakens, Daniël. (2017). [Equivalence Tests: A Practical Primer for t Tests, Correlations, and Meta-Analyses](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/80918653/lakeequi2017.pdf). Social Psychological and Personality Science, 8(4), 355–362.**

The primer explains two one-sided tests and setting equivalence bounds from the smallest effect size of interest. At a 5% level, equivalence under the standard TOST formulation corresponds to the 90% confidence interval lying inside both bounds. A non-inferiority question uses the relevant one-sided bound. ReviScope needs substantively justified margins and clustered inference; a nonsignificant specialist–baseline difference cannot establish comparable performance.

**34. Hayes, Andrew F., and Klaus Krippendorff. (2007). [Answering the Call for a Standard Reliability Measure for Coding Data](https://chukharev.github.io/520/Hayes_Krippendorff_2007.pdf). Communication Methods and Measures, 1(1), 77–89.**

The authors explain α across measurement levels, multiple observers, and incomplete rating matrices, and discuss interval estimation. Their worked example yields a 95% interval of approximately 0.708–0.808, illustrating that acceptability depends on the required reliability level. For ReviScope, support categories and consequence ratings require different distance functions. Report uncertainty and pre-adjudication agreement; consensus labels cannot be used to demonstrate independent rater reliability.

**35. Vach, Werner, and Oke Gerke. (2023). [Gwet’s AC1 is not a substitute for Cohen’s kappa—A comparison of basic properties](https://findresearcher.sdu.dk/ws/files/232856519/1_s2.0_S2215016123002108_main.pdf). MethodsX, 10, 102212.**

The authors analytically compare the chance comparators and prevalence dependence of κ and AC1. For fixed observed agreement, the coefficients can move in opposite directions as prevalence becomes imbalanced; AC1 can also be nonzero when raters are independent. They caution against interpreting AC1 through conventional κ labels. ReviScope should therefore report AC1 transparently alongside raw agreement, prevalence, and α, rather than selecting whichever coefficient appears most reassuring.

### (c) Open debates

- **Does judging require less capability than generating?** Verification can exploit references or independently answered subquestions, but difficult judge benchmarks show that recognising correctness remains demanding. The relevant question is whether ReviScope’s verifier has an informational or computational advantage over its generator, and whether that advantage improves decisions on actual criticism candidates. [Tan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12784v2), [Dhuliawala et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11495v2)

- **Can consensus establish validity?** Panels can improve accuracy and cost, yet shared training, styles, and blind spots can produce correlated agreement. Likewise, checklist agreement establishes consistency more readily than epistemic correctness. Human-labelled challenge cases and representative adjudication remain necessary. [Verga et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796), [Lee et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.796/)

- **What is the correct criticism unit?** Excessive splitting can inflate counts and sever inferential context; excessive merging can conceal distinct problems. Entailment helps identify relationships but itself makes errors. Issue identity, factual support, consequence, and remedy equivalence should be separate annotation decisions. [Gunjal and Durrett](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.215/)

- **How much ground truth is available?** Planted keys provide finite recall denominators, but human reviews and pooled system outputs are incomplete discovery processes. A pooled adjudicated set can estimate relative coverage, yet cannot establish recall of every valid criticism. ReviScope should explicitly distinguish benchmark recall from coverage of an adjudicated pool. [Liang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01783v1)

- **Does specialist decomposition earn its coordination cost?** Positive debate and verification results coexist with strong single-agent baselines and matched-budget findings favouring single agents. Scientific review may benefit from external tools and separable expertise, but the reviewed studies do not establish that transfer. Stage-level findings and cost–quality curves can identify where specialists help. [Du et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14325v1), [Wang et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.331/), [Tran and Kiela](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02460v1)

- **How many papers, runs, raters, and items are enough?** These address different uncertainties: papers support generalisation, runs estimate generation variability, and independent raters estimate annotation reproducibility. The answer depends on the decision margin, prevalence, clustering, and variance components. A pilot can support a defensible allocation; it cannot turn a very small paper sample into a conclusive architecture comparison. [Westfall et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25111580/), [Kim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16354)
