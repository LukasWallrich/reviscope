# Alpha pilot evaluation

This pilot demonstrates that the full evaluation workflow runs on real, version-matched manuscripts and public human reviews. It is a development result, not a validation claim.

Two Meta-Psychology round-one submissions were paired with the review written against that exact version. Binary hashes and extraction provenance are recorded in the corpus manifest and the local `runs/eval/real/provenance.json`. Decision letters, rebuttals, and later review rounds were excluded.

The development pipeline used `gpt-5.6-luna` at maximum effort with the version-one quantitative-social-science profile. Each candidate review was compared with one public human review twice, reversing A/B order. A Claude-family Fable judge at high effort and an OpenAI-family `gpt-5.6-sol` judge at high effort independently evaluated correctness, importance, specificity, grounding, usefulness, and actionability.

For both papers, both judges preferred the pipeline review in both presentation orders. Fable confidence ranged from 0.58 to 0.78; Sol confidence ranged from 0.96 to 0.99. Thus the paper-level result is 2/2 candidate preferences under each judge family, with no order disagreements or invalid judgments. With only two papers, the Wilson 95% interval for a 2/2 proportion is approximately 0.34 to 1.00. The exact rationales and criterion-level assessments remain in the ignored local result artifacts referenced by `eval/results/alpha-pilot.v1.json`.

The sample is unusually narrow: both papers concern versions of z-curve, both share an author, both are methods-heavy, and each comparison uses one human reviewer rather than the complete review process. Model judges may also prefer model-generated structure or coverage. The defensible claim is that the pipeline review was preferred on these two development cases by the configured judges. This does not establish human-level review or performance across psychology and the social sciences.

The final alpha-v2 profile was also run on the second paper. Luna at maximum effort generated 21 candidate findings; the editorial and verification gates published five (three major and two minor), with all five source and remedy assessments supported by a separate-family Fable verifier at high effort. Against the same public human review, Fable preferred the human review in both orders (confidence 0.60 and 0.55), while Sol preferred the alpha review in both orders (0.91 and 0.86). The predefined cross-judge aggregation therefore records a tie. This is a profile variant of an existing paper, not a third independent paper. The disagreement is useful evidence that model-judge conclusions are sensitive to judge family even when each judge is internally order-stable.

The final run reused cached Luna generation stages after source-gating changes and reran Fable verification (337.5 seconds) and reconciled editorial synthesis (314.1 seconds). The original generation-stage durations and the final refresh provenance are recorded in the compact result JSON.

On one separately generated Dawes benchmark case containing ten planted errors, with the manuscript and labels kept separate during review generation, both the development baseline and alpha-v2 pipeline recovered four errors under strict Fable adjudication. This does not establish that the case was absent from model training or untouched during broader development. Alpha v2 reduced published concerns from 12 to 5 while retaining all four detected errors. One baseline error was uncertain; none was uncertain under v2. This is one paper and measures sensitivity to known planted errors, not false-positive prevalence. A follow-up intake audit found that the supplied paper-8 text retained the benchmark banner declaring ten planted errors. Specific labels were withheld, but the presence and count of errors were disclosed to the reviewer. See [the detailed trace](pilot-error-trace-2026-09-07.md).

Fable assessed all 24 published findings across the two development reviews as supported. The final deterministic source gate anchored every cited excerpt for 17 findings; seven remained unresolved because at least one model-selected excerpt could not be anchored after a bounded repair. No finding was classified as contradicted. These results demonstrate the verifier and fail-closed source gate, but they do not measure a false-claim rate: all external substantive audit verdicts came from Fable, with no human adjudication, and unresolved evidence is not evidence of a false claim.

Synthetic verifier controls are listed separately and never contribute to prevalence or recall estimates. Early partial audit artifacts were migrated once after manually checking that their manuscript, finding set, prompt, and backend were unchanged. Current code requires an exact frozen verification configuration and rejects legacy partial outputs, changed content, or cached finding IDs outside the current input.

## Review representation sensitivity

The implemented normalization experiment converts each review into atomic assessments using GLM-5.3-Flash, then checks source-span identity and obtains a Fable high audit of faithfulness and completeness. It preserves positive assessments, qualifications, mathematical detail and absent remedies, rather than asking for a shorter or improved review. Every input receives the same schema and at most one audit-guided revision. A failed input makes the complete normalized comparison ineligible; it is not dropped to obtain a surviving subset.

The development diagnostic is recorded in `eval/results/normalized-review-diagnostic.v1.json`. Across nine source reviews, four inventories passed, three failed final fidelity or duplication gates, and two final audits were unavailable after Claude quota exhaustion. The latter are incomplete rather than failed fidelity judgments. Completed GLM calls reported $0.040997075 in total usage; unreturned failed/cancelled usage is not included. Both final-V2 Z-curve comparison inputs failed the strict final gate, so no normalized Z-curve comparison was run. Three of the four PeerJ human inventories passed; the remaining failure blocks the normalized seven-review ranking. These are representation-fidelity failures, not findings that the source human reviews contain false claims. Prompt revisions and initial/final audit versions are retained, so this is development evidence rather than a prospective model benchmark. Normalization can test sensitivity to presentation but cannot by itself establish an unbiased judge.

## Sampling population

The [open-review sampling frame](OPEN_REVIEW_SAMPLING_FRAME.md) and its linked primary-source audits distinguish public reports from public reviewed manuscripts. The target for the quantitative alpha is completed empirical social/personality psychology, defined by topic and design before archive accessibility. Mainstream psychology journals with transparent review are useful discovery strata, but many do not publish the original submission. Exact round-matched files and externally matched preprint proxies are analysed separately. Health-heavy archives and postpublication review models do not substitute for a discipline-relevant population sample.

The already inspected PeerJ 236 case is a workflow diagnostic. It was demoted after assessment of the human comparator set, so its selection cannot contribute to a future preregistered competitiveness estimate. All four first-round reports remain separate comparators. A future random sample must freeze eligibility before human-review quality or AI agreement is inspected; a demanding challenge panel can be defined in advance and reported separately.

## Seven-review empirical workflow diagnostic

Three full `social_psychology_v2` reviews of Nettle et al.'s original PeerJ 236 submission completed with maximum generation effort. Luna produced 24 candidates and published eight findings (14 merged, two held for review); Sol produced 22 and published six (11 merged, five rejected); Opus produced 25 and published ten (14 merged, one held for review). All stages completed with `partial=false`. Every published finding had anchored quotations, `llm_supported` claim status and a supported remedy. Fable high supplied product verification for all three: a different family for Luna/Sol and a different model in the same family for Opus. The comparison therefore concerns configured pipelines, not isolated generator models or independently verified ground truth.

The original-format pool contains all four first-round human reports and the three pipeline reviews. Codex Sol high and agy Gemini 3.8 Flash (High) each judged three seeded shuffled presentations, allowing ties and assessing six substantive criteria. The attempted Fable ranking produced no valid judgments after quota became unavailable and contributes no ranking evidence. These six presentations are repeated measurements of one paper. Sol is also a generator in the pool; blinding labels does not remove possible family or writing-style preferences. The agy judge ran in a sandbox with the full evidence supplied inline and an instruction not to use tools or browse. Its CLI does not expose the same mechanical tool-disable control as the manuscript-generation backends, so this restriction is recorded as prompt-level.

Both judges placed Opus first in every presentation and placed each pipeline review above each human review. Sol tied Luna and Sol in all three presentations; Gemini preferred Sol to Luna twice and tied them once. Human-review ordering also differed between judges. The full descriptive rank distributions and provenance are retained in `eval/results/peerj236-workflow-diagnostic.v1.json`, with a side-by-side table in the public report. No normalized seven-review ranking is reported because a required human inventory failed its fidelity gate. These observations do not rehabilitate the weak-comparator convenience case into a competitiveness estimate.
