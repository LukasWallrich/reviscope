Social-science review alpha

A transparent first test of a modular review pipeline for quantitative social science. The alpha produced source-anchored criticism, but the pilot is too small and too model-dependent to claim human-equivalent peer review.

What was observed.

The pipeline ran end to end on real papers. Two configured LLM judges preferred its development reviews to the paired human reviews, while the final-profile comparison split by judge. The final profile retained four detected planted errors while publishing fewer findings. The small, selected corpus and model-based assessment prevent a general performance or human-equivalence claim.

Final v2 produced a judge split.

On the second paper, Fable preferred the human review for deeper mathematical scrutiny, while noting that v2 was more careful and better sourced. Sol preferred v2 for its source accuracy, internal-consistency checks, and bounded remedies. Both judgments were stable across reversed presentation order. The predefined cross-judge result is a tie.

PeerJ. A resident survey and randomized neighbourhood field experiment measuring trust and paranoia. Luna, Sol, and Opus each reviewed the same original submission through the full pipeline. They published 8, 6, and 10 findings respectively. Every published finding was llm_supported, carried a supported remedy, and retained deterministic source offsets; all runs were complete rather than partial.

Four individual human reviews remained separate, producing a seven-review pool rather than a synthetic human consensus.

This is a purposive policy map, not a world-complete journal census. Most documented schemes cover accepted papers, so even an expanded corpus will remain conditioned on publication unless rejected submissions and their reports are released separately.

The target evaluation population should be defined first by social-science discipline, topic, study design, and manuscript maturity. Journals with convenient complete archives form an accessibility sample, not a substitute for that target. Mainstream psychology and social-science venues remain an acquisition challenge because many publish reports without the reviewed manuscript, or publish neither; they should not be replaced by arbitrary quotas from health-adjacent sources.

Pragmatic preprint matching for the exploratory alpha.

Search first within ±7 days of submission for the review round being evaluated, then select only a version posted before that round's earliest dated report or decision. For transferred papers, match each journal's reports to the version submitted to that journal. The seven-day window is a discovery heuristic, not an identity test. Audit authors, studies, samples, design, primary outcomes, central facts and claims, tables and figures, supplements, and reviewer-cited locations. Positive concordance is required; reviewer-driven differences in the final paper are expected when the exchange explains them. Exclude substantive mismatches before model calls. Before generation, freeze a version-difference map and apply it symmetrically during scoring; version-dependent points become unevaluable, and a material loss of comparability prevents a ranking. Report the default ±7-day analysis and ±30-day sensitivity separately. A strongly workflow-linked older version may enter a labelled wider-window stratum after the same audit. Exact and audited-proxy results remain separate, with an exact-only sensitivity analysis; inconclusive temporal matches do not enter performance estimates.

PeerJ supplied the clean version match

PeerJ is a broad multidisciplinary journal rather than a flagship psychology journal. Its public history exposed four reports and the original submitted manuscript, making a fair same-version comparison possible.

Communications Psychology is a candidate for a future product check

Its transparent-review bundles are promising and the journal is closer to the desired mainstream psychology setting. For the identified empirical article, we did not locate and verify the reviewed Version 0 manuscript. Comparing those reviews with the final published paper would confound review quality with author revisions, so it was excluded from human-review comparison.

Nature Human Behaviour and Nature Communications are stronger mainstream candidates for the next corpus round because they support transparent review and publish relevant human-behaviour or social-science research. Their pre-review manuscripts still require exact matching or a documented substantive proxy audit. A current Nature Human Behaviour discovery lead has a Research Square version posted 17 days after the displayed received date, illustrating why ±7 days cannot be a rigid cutoff; it is not admitted because its route, supplement completeness, and substantive identity remain unaudited. Psychological Science emphasizes research transparency, but the audited current guidance does not promise an equivalent public report corpus.

The two Meta-Psychology methods papers and the PeerJ convenience case do not represent social psychology. The former supplied the completed comparison pilot; the latter adds a workflow diagnostic, not a new competitiveness sample.

What changed from v1 to final v2

12 → 5

Fewer published concerns

On the shared second paper, v1 published 12 findings. V2 generated 21 candidates and published five after different-family verification and editorial gating: three major and two minor.

4 → 4

Planted-error recall retained

On the known-error case, v2 reduced the published set from 12 to five without losing any of the four strictly matched errors. This is promising restraint on one case, not an estimated precision gain.

The two human-review wins are development-baseline v1 results. Final v2 was compared on only the second paper, where the judge families split. V2 is therefore not credited with the v1 result.

What would change the conclusion

The next study should test the most plausible alternative explanations before expanding the claim.

Observed · feasibility failure

AI-assisted review normalization did not clear its gate

Four of nine inventories had passing final audits: PeerJ human reviews 1, 3, and 4, plus Luna's 14-assessment pipeline review. Three failed final gates: the two Z-curve reviews and PeerJ human review 2, which retained one assessment that did not faithfully preserve its source after revision. Sol's pipeline inventory was revised from 18 to 17 assessments but could not be re-audited after quota exhaustion; Opus's 18-assessment inventory could not be audited. Those two incomplete audits are not counted as passes. No normalized comparison or ranking was run. The development audit moved from prompt v2 to the stricter v3 while failures were being diagnosed, so this is an engineering diagnostic rather than a preregistered performance estimate.

Measured completed calls cost $0.041. Usage for failed or cancelled calls was not returned; a sub-$0.01 figure from configured token allowances is an estimate, not an observed charge or hard billing bound.

Observed · not estimable

The normalized seven-review comparison did not proceed

PeerJ human review 2 failed after its allowed revision, which alone blocked the full normalized pool. Sol's final audit and Opus's audit were also unavailable. The protocol did not rank a reduced passing subset. The original-format ranking above is reported separately and normalization remains a failed feasibility diagnostic.

Planned

Add human claim auditing

Commission blinded human assessment of a seeded random sample of published findings. Keep targeted audits of major and unresolved findings separate from prevalence estimates.

Planned

Expand beyond the z-curve cluster

Build a frozen multi-topic corpus with multiple reviews per manuscript, preregister the aggregation rule, and evaluate at the paper level. Order swaps and multiple judges remain repeated measurements, not extra papers.

Limits to keep visible

Small and selected evidence base

The historical comparison has two related z-curve methods papers sharing an author. PeerJ adds one empirical workflow diagnostic, not a representative third competitiveness case.

Model-based assessment

LLM judges can prefer model-generated style. Fable supplied the historical finding-audit verdicts and verified the three PeerJ pipeline reviews; the PeerJ ranking uses Gemini and Sol. Deterministic matching verifies quotation provenance, not the criticism's truth.

Uneven human comparators

Each methods paper was compared with one reviewer. PeerJ provides four separate reports, but that comparator set was judged insufficiently demanding for a competitiveness evaluation. Neither represents the full editorial process or a human consensus.

Reproducibility and credit

The recorded protocol swaps review order, aggregates swaps within judge and judges within paper, permits ties, and preserves hashes for source and result artifacts. A compact, machine-readable result record is maintained with the project.
