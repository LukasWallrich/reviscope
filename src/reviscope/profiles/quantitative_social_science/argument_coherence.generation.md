Infer the manuscript's genre first; tutorials, protocols and descriptive work have different argument chains than confirmatory studies, and a missing hypothesis is not in itself a defect in exploratory or descriptive work. This module owns the coherence of the whole argument: research question, theory, hypotheses or predictions, design, analysis, results and conclusions. Other modules check single steps within their own area; here, check whether the steps connect into one argument.

Start from the study map's question and claimed contribution. Reconstruct the central argument as a short chain and record each consequential link in argument_map: the located claim, the quoted premises that support it, the inferential step, and any quoted context that qualifies or defeats it. Record links that hold as well as links that fail. The map is a working record, not a finding, and there is no quota of links or findings.

For argument_chain, identify the weakest consequential link in the reconstructed chain and whether the conclusion depends on it. Say which connection fails: a missing premise, a false premise, circular support, or evidence compatible with several explanations.

For hypothesis_derivation, check whether each hypothesis or prediction follows from the stated theory or argument, whether it contradicts that argument, and whether competing theories the manuscript discusses or that are standard in the field would predict the same result. A prediction shared by the rival accounts cannot discriminate between them.

For conclusion_support, check whether the main conclusions follow from the reported results through the stated design, and whether the design can tell apart the explanations the manuscript discusses. Check single numerical or causal claims only as far as the whole-argument conclusion depends on them; individual result-to-claim wording belongs to interpretation.

For concept_stability, check whether a key construct keeps the same meaning from its theoretical definition, through its measure or manipulation, to the conclusions. Flag a construct that silently shifts meaning, or a concept used differently from the theory it is attributed to. Labels for variables, conditions and units belong to consistency.

For internal_contradiction, look for substantive claims in different sections that cannot both be true, such as a theoretical commitment in the introduction that the discussion contradicts, or a limitation that a later conclusion ignores. Numerical and categorical mismatches belong to consistency.

Quote both ends of every broken link. Recommend the smallest change that restores coherence, such as rescoping a conclusion, stating a missing premise or acknowledging a rival prediction; do not demand new studies when wording or framing resolves the problem.
