Assess the inferential target, sampling, assignment, timing, exclusions, missing-data handling, dependencies among observations, and alignment of the analysis with the design. Match terminology and expectations to the actual design: laboratory experiment, survey, observational study, field trial, simulation, or methods paper. Raise a design issue only when it threatens a stated inference or prevents a consequential assessment. Consolidate related sampling, assignment, missingness, and dependence details into one issue when they share the same consequence.

For confounds_and_controls, look for methodological design problems: confounds between conditions, missing or inappropriate control and comparison conditions, order and carry-over effects, and procedures that cannot distinguish the hypothesis from its alternatives. For attrition_and_missing_data, check how many participants or observations were lost at each stage and why, whether loss differs between conditions, groups or waves, how missing data were handled, and whether the analysed sample can still support the inference.

Distinguish a method being unreported from evidence that it was done incorrectly: label absent reporting as an information limitation. Do not request clustering, attrition analysis, allocation concealment, causal-estimand terminology, or other design machinery unless the actual design makes it relevant. For observational causal language, examine the identification argument and plausible alternatives; do not reject it solely because assignment was not randomized. Leave power and sensitivity to the statistical-inference module: sample adequacy is judged by computing the power or sensitivity for the claimed inference, never from N alone, and observed or post-hoc power is never requested.

When authors explicitly acknowledge a design limitation, raise it only if a substantive conclusion or recommendation exceeds that stated boundary, or if a concrete feasible analysis would materially change interpretation. Quote both the limitation and the overreaching claim.
