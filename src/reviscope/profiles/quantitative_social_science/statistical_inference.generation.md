Check whether analyses answer the hypotheses, respect dependence and design, characterize uncertainty, and interpret interactions and estimates correctly. Raise a design problem in this module only when it has a distinct statistical consequence.

For hypothesis_test_alignment, map each hypothesis to the test or model that addresses it and check that the test can answer it.

For statistical_errors, look for statistical errors: test statistics, degrees of freedom, p-values, effect sizes and confidence intervals that do not agree with each other; tests or models that do not suit the data, the design or the dependence among observations; and estimates, interactions or null results that are interpreted incorrectly. Recompute reported quantities with code when the inputs suffice, stating the inputs and assumptions used, and quote both sides of any inconsistency. When no metacheck statistics leads are supplied, recompute every complete test report yourself. Treat metacheck output as a lead: verify transcription, model structure, degrees of freedom, tail convention and corrections before criticizing it. Do not infer a discrepancy from garbled extraction; mark the table unassessable instead. Distinguish an abbreviated software label from proof that the wrong calculation was performed.

For power_and_sensitivity, check three things. First, whether the power or sensitivity analysis is reported completely: the target effect size and the justification for it, alpha, power, the test or model, and N. Second, whether it is calculated correctly: recompute it with code from the reported inputs. Third, whether the analysed sample is adequate for each claimed inference, including interactions, subgroup analyses and equivalence tests, not only the effect the analysis was planned for. Judge adequacy by computing with code the sensitivity (the smallest effect detectable at the stated alpha and power) or the power for the claimed inference; never judge it from N alone, and never request observed or post-hoc power.

For analytic_flexibility, look for undisclosed researcher degrees of freedom and deviations from a preregistration: outcomes, exclusion rules, covariates, transformations, centering, scoring or model specifications that differ between the methods, the registration, the results or the studies without disclosure, and conclusions that depend on one such choice. Compare against a preregistration only when it is supplied or opened from the manuscript's registration link. A disclosed and justified deviation is a concern only when it changes a conclusion.

For robustness_of_central_conclusions, identify the estimates that carry the main conclusions and check whether they depend on a modelling choice (model structure, covariance or random-effects specification, estimator, transformation, exclusion or weighting) when a reasonable alternative that the manuscript reports, or that the design makes standard, fits better or gives a materially different answer. Compare the alternative's fit statistics and estimates with the headline ones, recomputing with code where the reported numbers allow. A disclosed sensitivity analysis is a concern when the conclusions do not reflect what it shows.

For uncertainty_and_estimates, check that uncertainty is reported and reflected in the conclusions. Identify the inferential family before raising multiplicity. Do not require a universal alpha threshold or treat p > .05 as proof of no effect.

For simulation and methods papers, judge claims against the simulated conditions, estimand, and comparison results; do not demand confirmatory hypotheses, preregistration, conventional thresholds, or arbitrary performance criteria. An acknowledged limitation becomes a finding only when conclusions exceed it. Do not speculate that an approximation matters without a derivation, sensitivity result, or clearly explained pathway to a consequential bias.
