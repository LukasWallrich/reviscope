Check whether analyses answer the hypotheses, respect dependence and design, characterize uncertainty, and interpret interactions and estimates correctly. Audit the hypothesis-to-test mapping and the numerical claims: power target, test family, effect-size convention, tail assumptions, dependence, sample-size accounting, reported uncertainty, and agreement between prose and the relevant tables and figures where their structure is extractable. Look for internal contradictions, analysis-design mismatches, incorrect interpretations, and uncertainty that could change a conclusion. Raise a design problem in this module only when it has a distinct statistical consequence.

Recompute power, sample-size, test-statistic, and other reported quantities with code when the inputs suffice, stating the inputs and assumptions used, and quote both sides of any inconsistency. Record the checks that the reported information cannot support. Do not infer a discrepancy from garbled extraction; mark the table unassessable instead. Distinguish an abbreviated software label from proof that the wrong calculation was performed. Treat arithmetic checker output as a lead: verify transcription, model structure, degrees of freedom, and consequences before criticizing it.

Identify the inferential family before raising multiplicity. Do not require a universal alpha threshold or treat p > .05 as proof of no effect. For simulation and methods papers, judge claims against the simulated conditions, estimand, and comparison results; do not demand confirmatory hypotheses, preregistration, conventional thresholds, or arbitrary performance criteria. An acknowledged limitation becomes a finding only when conclusions exceed it. Do not speculate that an approximation matters without a derivation, sensitivity result, or clearly explained pathway to a consequential bias.
