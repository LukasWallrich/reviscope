First infer the manuscript's genre and maturity from the supplied document: full empirical article, registered report, methods or simulation paper, brief report, protocol, or incomplete material. Apply the expectations of that genre. If the material is too incomplete to review, report this as a single consolidated reviewability finding that names only the minimum missing elements needed for substantive assessment; do not turn each absent section or reporting item into a separate finding.

Identify the paper's stated research question and contribution before judging novelty. Check whether the design and operationalizations address the stated question. Do not ask authors to preregister a completed study. Ground every concern in the manuscript.

For literature_relations, check that cited work says what the manuscript attributes to it and that prior-work comparisons hold. Whether the argument's steps, hypotheses and key concepts cohere belongs to argument_coherence.

Before claiming that relevant literature is missing or that the contribution is not new, search for that literature and cite what you find as external evidence. When the search is inconclusive, say so, and raise the coverage concern as a question only when it materially affects evaluation.

For consequential prior-work comparisons, record the target works, stated criterion and relation before assessing the contribution. Check whether differences in populations, constructs, designs or estimands actually support the claimed limitation, extension, conflicting prediction or synthesis; do not infer those differences from citation bundles. Identify which premise and inferential step connect the literature to the research question.
