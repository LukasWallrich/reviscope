Return a disposition with a reason for every candidate: keep, merge, reject, or needs_review. Keep every distinct, specific finding that verification supports. The number of published findings is not limited, and a finding is never set aside because of how many others there are.

Group candidates semantically by the underlying defect and consequence across modules, even when wording and evidence differ. Select as canonical the supported candidate whose existing wording, evidence and remedy are most proportionate, and merge every duplicate into it. A merge target must have a keep disposition and evidential support at least as strong as the source. You cannot rewrite substantive text. Supported merged claims and their anchored quotations are attached to the surviving finding without rewriting. If no candidate in a semantic group is proportionately stated, set the group aside rather than claiming to recalibrate its wording.

Use each finding's final status to assess support: llm_supported, supported, recomputed and verified_deterministic are publishable evidence states. verifier_status=supported alone is insufficient when final status is unresolved or unverified. Never merge a supported finding into an unresolved, unverified or contradicted target. If a proposed canonical target is not publishable, choose a supported member as canonical or keep the supported finding separately. Do not lose a supported criticism because a related candidate failed verification or external-source checks.

Assign final severity and a positive integer priority to every keep decision with all findings in view. Lower priority numbers come first within each severity. Discovery severity is provisional and retained for audit. Critical means a demonstrated error that invalidates a central result. Major means a supported issue that could change a central conclusion or its interpretation, an internal inconsistency affecting a reported result, or a missing analysis or information without which a central claim cannot be assessed. Minor means a local, presentational or reporting issue without such consequence. Missing information is major only when a central claim cannot be assessed without it. Severity reflects consequence; verification status determines evidential support and eligibility. Uncertainty, acknowledged scope limitations and requests for broader validation are not critical. A review with no major findings is acceptable.

Never reject a finding merely because it criticizes a standard practice or uses a firm tone. An overreaching remedy does not invalidate an otherwise supported criticism: keep the criticism eligible. The renderer withholds overreaching advice, so do not reject or flag the finding for that reason and do not invent a replacement. If the manuscript is not substantively reviewable, publish one concise triage finding rather than a rubric inventory. The audit record preserves every candidate and its disposition.
