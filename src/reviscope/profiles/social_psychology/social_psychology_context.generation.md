Assess social and psychological mechanisms, demand and expectancy explanations when they are concretely plausible, manipulation-to-construct alignment, participant and stimulus scope, and plausible alternative explanations. Evaluate whether the design distinguishes the claimed mechanism from alternatives. Raise a field-specific issue only when it materially affects a stated inference and is distinct from issues covered by the design, measurement, statistical-inference, and interpretation modules; keep it distinct from generic timing, scoring, and dependence issues. Consolidate related concerns that share a consequence into one issue rather than a checklist of possible concerns.

For people_and_stimuli, also consider the order of measures and manipulations: whether an earlier task, question or manipulation could prime, carry over into or change the meaning of a later one that a central inference uses.

For replication_and_original_comparison, first decide whether the manuscript replicates or directly builds on a specific original study; if not, mark the check not_applicable. For a replication, compare its population, materials, manipulation, measures, order, exclusion rules and analysis with the original, opening the original article when it is accessible, and identify deviations that change what the replication tests. Check that its success criteria (for example a small-telescopes test, equivalence bounds or an interval comparison) are stated before the results, applied correctly and reported consistently, and that comparisons with the original effect use comparable effect-size metrics and designs. Conclusions that the original effect failed to replicate, or replicated, must respect the uncertainty in both estimates. Do not demand an exact replication when the authors state and justify a conceptual or close replication.

Treat missing controls and exclusions as questions about a specific inference, not automatic defects. Assess reported manipulation checks without conditioning the main inference on post-treatment variables, and do not prescribe conditioning on post-treatment measures by default. Do not request manipulation checks, measurement invariance, reliability thresholds, or common-method diagnostics by default.

Treat preregistration as evidence only when it is provided. Its absence is not an error, and never request retrospective preregistration. Compare adherence only against the registration itself, either supplied or opened from the manuscript's registration link; a deviation is a concern only when it is consequential.
